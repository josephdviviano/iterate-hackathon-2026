## Post-mortem
<!-- For failures and missed predictions: what you predicted, what happened, the root cause,
what the world model got wrong, the lesson. -->
Predicted time [7.65, 8.05]; got 8.45. I extrapolated E017 (2000 -> 1536 was time-neutral per image) to 1024. Root cause: per-step fixed costs scale with the step count: Muon is ~2 ms of GPU time per step regardless of batch (E018 profile), so +172 steps add ~0.35 s; the Lookahead/SGD/augment-index overheads add a little more; and conv/BN kernels lose efficiency at 1024 (especially the small 8x8/4x4 layers). At 1536 those fixed costs were ~7% of a step; at 1024 they are ~11%. The world model's 'batch is time-neutral down to ~1536' was correctly bounded, but I ignored that Muon's per-step cost is fixed. Lesson: per-image cost = conv cost + (Muon ~2 ms + overheads)/batch; below 1536 the second term grows fast. Any smaller-batch idea needs a cheaper Muon step first (fewer NS steps, or updating Muon every k steps).

## Post-mortem
<!-- For failures and missed predictions: what you predicted, what happened, the root cause,
what the world model got wrong, the lesson. -->
Predicted [0.7480, 0.7550]; got 0.7349. I leaned on a random-Gaussian singular-value check, where one preconditioned step already lands singular values in [0.3-0.8, 1.2]. Root cause: real momentum-gradient matrices are strongly low-rank/skewed (a few large singular values, a long tail of tiny ones). After AOL rescaling plus a single quintic step, the tail directions stay far below 1, so the update is dominated by the top few directions: effectively it is closer to normalized SGD than to Muon, and Muon's benefit (~0.7 pp over SGD in E007, here more) is lost. The world model wrongly treated 'Muon is insensitive to singular-value spread in [0.67, 1.2]' (E024) as license to go further; the relevant quantity is how well the *small* singular values of real gradients are lifted, which needs >= 2 iterations. Lesson: validate orthogonalization approximations on real gradient matrices (captured from a training step), not on Gaussian matrices.

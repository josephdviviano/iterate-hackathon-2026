The marginal 32 px epoch (~0.85 s) buys about 0.7-1.0 pp, so a per-step cut is worth keeping only if its pp-per-second rate falls below that measured line. Every past 'better than the epoch line' verdict depends on this slope, which has only been estimated from an SGD pair and one noisy Muon pair.
H1 (open, c=0.75): Time is mainly set by sample efficiency: how many epochs/steps the recipe needs to reach >=75.3% (architecture, init, optimizer, schedule, label smoothing, augmentation). Recipes that need fewer epochs dominate the score.

## Post-mortem
<!-- For failures and missed predictions: what you predicted, what happened, the root cause,
what the world model got wrong, the lesson. -->
Predicted [0.7505, 0.7575]; got 0.7498, below the range, with +1 epoch of training. The world model assumed accuracy is monotone in epochs (at ~0.5-1 pp/epoch). Candidate root causes: (1) schedule coupling: everything is defined over total steps (triangle warmup = 23% of steps, Lookahead alpha ramp, whitening-bias epochs fixed at 5), so adding an epoch at the start shifts the peak-LR region into the low-res phase and changes which resolution the high-LR steps see; adding at the end (E032) lengthens the decay; neither is a pure 'more training' change. (2) noise: config-to-config SE ~0.2-0.33 pp; -0.27 pp is ~1 SE. (3) a genuine plateau: with ~9.5 full-res-equivalent epochs and Muon, the recipe may be near the accuracy this architecture/regularization reaches at this LR scale, so margin must come from capacity/regularization, not epochs. What the world model got wrong: treating the epoch slope measured at 9 full-res epochs without resizing (E009->E014) as valid for the resizing recipe. Lesson: epochs are no longer a dependable margin lever; margin should come from changes that alter the model/regularization (e.g. width in group 2/3 under the frozen-block regime, ls, momentum), each checked with 2 salts.

## Muon for small CIFAR CNNs: what the verified cards cover

**Coverage is thin.** All three verified cards come from one paper, Turbo-Muon (2025). That paper is about making the Newton-Schulz (NS) step cheaper. It does not tune Muon for CIFAR CNNs. No card gives a learning rate, momentum, batch size, weight normalization or update scaling. No card says which parameters to exclude or measures the speedup over Nesterov SGD at short schedules. For those settings the only evidence is still this loop's own E007, which used the airbench94_muon recipe: lr ~0.24, momentum 0.6, 3 NS steps, batch 2000, with biases, BN and the head kept on SGD. It reached 0.7544 in 8.71 s. That is the first feasible run, and it refuted H1.4.1.

**Cards**

- **C0008** (*untested*): preconditioning lets Muon drop one NS iteration, going from the usual 5 to 4. E007 already runs only 3 steps, so the paper's setting is not ours. The relevant gap is in the tree: no hypothesis says that the optimizer step (the NS orthogonalization) takes a measurable share of step time. **Action:** first profile the NS share of ms/step in E007. Test Turbo-style preconditioning with 2 NS steps only if that share is above ~5%. Note that the card was auto-mapped to H1.1.3; that mapping is wrong.
- **C0009** (*conditional*): the preconditioning is a drop-in change that needs no retuning. This holds for the paper's large-scale and speedrun setups. It has not been shown for 3-step Muon on 576-channel convs. **Action:** if you try it, keep E007's lr and momentum fixed so the test isolates the change.
- **C0010** (*conditional*): Turbo-Muon matches reference performance on "language and vision" tasks. The tasks, model sizes and schedule lengths are not specified. It says nothing about label smoothing, so its auto-mapping to H1.3 is also wrong. Treat it only as weak support that cutting NS steps will not cost accuracy. Check that on our own runs.

**How this bears on the loop**

- E007's margin is 0.11 pp above the 0.753 threshold. Under the H1.1.3 noise model, the 3-seed mean moves by up to ~0.2 pp, so feasibility is not secure. Before spending on micro-speedups, replicate E007 once.
- Muon now beats SGD at about equal wall time. So H1.4 (Nesterov SGD beats Muon here) should drop in confidence, and Muon tuning deserves its own Lvl2 node. Its predictions would cover:
  - fewer epochs at Muon's higher accuracy, trading the ~0.1 pp margin for time;
  - momentum (0.6 vs 0.85–0.95) and the lr;
  - batch 2000 vs 1024;
  - whether the frozen whitening conv and the head stay excluded from Muon.
- The width-trimming runs (E008 and E009, both on SGD) were close to feasible. Repeating them on Muon is the most direct way to turn E007's accuracy gain into a lower time.

## Claims
- C0008 (untested; H1.4, H2): The tree has no node for the optimizer's NS cost as a share of step time. Profile the NS share in E007. If it is above ~5%, test preconditioned Muon with 2 NS steps. The auto-mapping to H1.1.3 is wrong.
- C0009 (conditional; H1.4.1): Shown only as a drop-in for standard 5-step Muon. If tested here, keep E007's lr 0.24, momentum 0.6 and batch 2000 fixed to isolate the effect.
- C0010 (conditional; H1.4): Weak, unspecified evidence that cutting NS steps keeps accuracy. It has nothing to do with label smoothing (the H1.3 mapping is wrong). Check any NS-step cut against the 0.753 threshold with replicates.

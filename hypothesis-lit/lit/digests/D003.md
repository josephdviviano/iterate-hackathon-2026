## Progressive resizing: what the cards say and what applies to this loop

**Bottom line:** the cards give no numbers for accuracy cost at matched epochs, no best low-res fraction, and nothing on BN or FixRes handling of the train/test resolution gap. Your own runs answer the first question better than the literature does.

**Your own data (compared against the incumbent at each run):**
- **E011:** 50% of epochs at 24 px with no extra epochs. Accuracy fell 0.84 pp (0.7460 vs 0.7544) and time fell 21%.
- **E015:** 10.5 epochs instead of 9.0. Accuracy was 0.28 pp lower and time 12.5% lower.
- **E020:** 11 epochs instead of 9.5, on the E018 graphed base. Accuracy rose 0.17 pp and time fell 4.2% (0.7588 @ 7.82 s).

So spending about half the epochs at 24 px costs roughly 0.8 pp, and about +1.5 epochs buys it back.

**What the literature adds**
- **C0011 (EfficientNetV2): explains a result already seen.** Progressive resizing on its own lowers accuracy, which matches E011. It gives no magnitude, so it adds nothing beyond E011.
- **C0012 (EfficientNetV2): a mechanism the tree lacks (untested).** It reports that scaling regularization with image size recovers the loss without giving up the speed. Weaker regularization at small sizes, stronger at full size. Right now the loop pays for the low-res phase only with extra epochs. Suggested follow-up: an H2.3 child that tests a per-phase regularization schedule on E020 at a fixed 11 epochs. In the 24 px phase, use smaller crop translation (scaled to 24 px, or none) and label smoothing around 0.2. In the 32 px phase, use 0.3. Prediction: at least +0.15 pp, which would then allow cutting about 0.3 epochs.
- **C0013 (EfficientNetV2 SOTA claim): conditional.** It applies to ImageNet scale with long schedules. It doesn't bear on a 10-epoch CIFAR-100 run, so park it.
- **C0014–C0016 (UltraLight+, 64→96 px on CIFAR-10) and C0017–C0018 (DenseNet rice leaves, 224→320→384): conditional and weak.** They use different resolutions and tasks, and none compares at matched epochs or matched time. Don't use them to tune anything.

**Which schedule and what low-res fraction**
No card answers this. H2.3.1's three-step schedule (24 → 28 → 32 px) remains untested. The 28 px step is the cheap next sweep. Separately, try 40% vs 60% low-res on E020 to find the best fraction, judged on pp per second against the E021 epoch slope.

**Train/test resolution gap**
No card covers this. From my own knowledge, not a card: FixRes-type problems mostly come up when training ends below test resolution. E020's last 6 epochs run at 32 px, so BN statistics and receptive-field scale are already re-fitted at test resolution. BN re-estimation or a FixRes fine-tune is low priority and shouldn't get a run unless a schedule ends at low resolution.

## Claims
- C0011 (explains; H2.3, H2.3.1): Explains the E011 drop (0.84 pp) when progressive resizing ran with no compensating epochs. It gives no magnitude, so treat E011/E015/E020 as the loop's own exchange rate.
- C0012 (untested; H2.3, H1.3): Add an H2.3 child: on E020 at a fixed 11 epochs, scale regularization with resolution (less crop translation and ls ~0.2 at 24 px, ls 0.3 at 32 px). Predict >=+0.15 pp, which would allow an epoch cut.
- C0013 (conditional; H2.3): Holds only for ImageNet-scale, long-schedule training. Park it; no action for 10-epoch CIFAR-100.
- C0014 (conditional; H2.3): Different regime (64->96 px, tiny edge net, CIFAR-10) and no matched-epoch comparison. Don't use it to tune.
- C0018 (conditional; H2.3): Different domain and resolutions, with no epoch-matched baseline. Ignore it for schedule choice.

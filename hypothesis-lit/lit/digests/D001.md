# D001: What architectures and training recipes reach >=75% CIFAR-100 top-1 (single view, no TTA) in the fewest epochs or least GPU wall time, and how does accuracy scale with epochs and width for ResNet-9/airbench-style networks on CIFAR-100?

Request R001 (agent, after E000); 80 papers found, 3 relevant, 3 read in full, 7 verified claims; cost $0.54

## Literature digest: fast CIFAR-100 to ≥75% (no TTA)

**Bottom line:** none of the verified cards covers the regime this loop is in: an airbench-style net trained for ≤10 epochs and taking about 10 s. All the cards use ResNet-56 or ResNet-110 with schedules of 25+ epochs and wall-clock times of thousands of seconds. None of them gives epoch or width scaling for ResNet-9 or airbench on CIFAR-100. The loop's own results are the best evidence available.

**What the cards say**
- **C0001 / C0002 (conditional, H1.1):** on ResNet-56 at batch 512, a 1cycle LR schedule beats step schedules, with higher accuracy at 25 epochs than step reaches at any epoch count. This fits the triangular schedule the recipe already uses, but nothing was tested below 25 epochs. Keep the triangular schedule and don't spend runs on schedule shape. C0002's mapping to H1.2 (flip) is wrong: the card is about the LR schedule.
- **C0004 / C0005 (conditional):** SAM variants on ResNet-110 take 3000+ s to reach 82%. SAM roughly doubles the cost of each step, so it is the wrong trade at a 75% target. Don't pursue it.
- **C0006 (conditional, H1.4):** a Muon-refined run reaches an 83% tier that no SGD arm reaches. That is a ceiling effect far above 75%, so it doesn't contradict H1.4 at this target. Leave H1.4.1 as a low-priority test.
- **C0007 (conditional, H1.4):** plain Nesterov SGD reaches 80% much faster than SAM or Muon-refined methods, which give no speedup below SGD's ceiling. This leans toward H1.4 (SGD beats Muon at modest targets), but on ResNet-110 at long schedules.
- **C0003 (untested):** a normalization-free ResNet init reports "competitive" results with no number. It isn't actionable here; skip it.

**What the loop's own data says (more useful than the cards)**
- **Epoch scaling:** going from 8.5 to 9.5 epochs raised accuracy from 0.7383 to 0.7482 (about +1.0 pp per epoch) for about +1 s. The E003 replicate gave exactly the same mean accuracy (0.7482), so the 3-seed run looks deterministic and the run-to-run noise floor is close to 0. That is not the same as the variance across 40 seeds.
- **Width scaling:** in E004, widths 128/384/768 at 7.5 epochs reached 0.7389 in 7.49 s. Widths 128/384/576 at 8.5 epochs reached 0.7383 in 8.04 s. Same accuracy for about 7% less time. H1.5.1 was refuted in the direction that **wider and shorter is more time-efficient**. The 768 net costs about 1.0 s per epoch against about 0.95 s for 576, and saves about one epoch.
- **Next step:** the target needs about +0.5 pp over the incumbent. Linear extrapolation says the 576 net needs about 10 epochs (about 9.5 s) and the 768 net about 8.5–9 epochs (about 8.7–9.0 s). Run **128/384/768 at 9.0 epochs** first. If it lands at 0.753 or above, step down by 0.25 epochs at a time. Also consider trying 896 in the last group.

## Claims
- C0001 (conditional; H1.1): 1cycle beats step LR at 25+ epochs on ResNet-56. This fits the triangular schedule already in use, but it says nothing about <=10 epochs. Keep the schedule and don't spend runs on its shape.
- C0002 (conditional; H1.1): Same finding as C0001. The card's mapping to H1.2 (flip) is wrong because the card is about the LR schedule. No action.
- C0005 (conditional; H1.1.2): SAM variants on ResNet-110 take thousands of seconds, and SAM doubles per-step cost, which is the wrong trade at a 75% target with a ~10 s score. Don't pursue SAM.
- C0006 (conditional; H1.4): Muon's advantage shows up as a ceiling far above 75% (83% vs 82.4%). It doesn't challenge H1.4 at this target. Keep H1.4.1 as a low-priority test.
- C0007 (conditional; H1.4): Plain Nesterov SGD reaches modest targets faster than SAM or Muon-refined methods. This weakly supports H1.4, but in a long-schedule ResNet-110 regime. Keep SGD for now.
- C0003 (untested; H1.1): Normalization-free init reports 'competitive' results with no number. Not actionable; skip it.

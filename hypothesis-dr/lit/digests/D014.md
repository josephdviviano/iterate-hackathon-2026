# D014: For very short training runs (a few epochs, CNN on CIFAR-scale images, Muon or SGD with warmup then linear decay plus weight averaging), how should the low-resolution phase and the batch size be scheduled together, and how should the lr be adjusted at each switch, so that cheap early steps save compute without costing final accuracy? Two results motivate this. E043: moving the 24->32 px boundary from 1.5 to 2.25 of 6.75 epochs cost about 0.48 pp, so low-res steps seem to hurt once they reach the peak-lr phase. E041: bs 1536 saved about 5% but left only a thin margin. Specific questions: (a) Do the 'multigrid' and 'progressive learning' literatures support raising the batch size during the low-res phase (constant FLOPs or constant pixels per step) and then dropping it at full res? (b) Should low-res training line up with the warmup? (c) Does the train/test resolution mismatch (FixRes) need a short full-res tail or a BN fix? (d) Is there evidence on critical batch size, or on ramping the batch size, for Muon-style normalized optimizers over short horizons?

Request R014 (auto, after E043); 80 papers found, 5 relevant, 5 read in full, 16 verified claims; cost $0.93

**Coverage.** The cards cover (a)–(c), but only from long-horizon ImageNet and video work. Nothing covers (d): no card addresses critical batch size or batch ramps for Muon-style normalized optimizers. Treat (d) as unanswered.

**(a) Raising the batch during the low-res phase.**
- The multigrid papers scale batch size and lr up together when resolution drops, which keeps per-step cost roughly constant. They report 3–6× speedups with no accuracy loss (C0184, C0185). These are 100+ epoch video runs, so the result is **conditional** here.
- For this loop, constant-FLOP batching would mean bs ≈1820 at 24 px, and therefore *fewer* steps inside a phase that already holds only ~23% of steps.
- The batch-size support depends on two things:
  - Linear lr scaling (C0187, C0189). That applies to the SGD group only. Muon updates are normalized, and E046 (Muon lr 0.20 at bs 1536) did not recover E041's loss.
  - Warmup (C0188).
- C0186 found that changing two size axes at the same switch was worse than nesting them in a hierarchy (spatial cycles containing temporal sub-cycles). That is an **untested** risk for any plan that switches resolution and batch size at the same moment. Change them at different step boundaries and run one ablation per change.
- Lowest-risk test: bs ~1536 during the 24 px phase only, with SGD-group lr ×1.5, Muon lr unchanged, and bs back to 1024 at 32 px.

**(b) Should low-res line up with warmup?**
- EfficientNetV2 keeps one global warmup-then-decay lr schedule and changes image size in four equal stages, with no lr adjustment at the switches (C0194, C0195). Low-res steps do run through peak lr there, so the literature does **not** support the idea that low-res is bad at peak lr. That **contradicts** the E043 reading only in the long-horizon regime.
- A simpler explanation for E043 is that it cut full-res epochs from 5.25 to 4.5 at a steep knee.
- The literature adds two pieces of guidance:
  - Small images want weaker augmentation (C0193). This is an **untested** lever: drop translate-2 and jitter during the 24 px phase. It fits under H1.8.2.
  - There is a minimum useful size (C0190). In that paper it was set by optical flow, so it is only weakly relevant to 24 px. It is consistent with the E044 28 px stage not paying.
- Keep the boundary at or before the end of warmup, and test reduced augmentation at 24 px.

**(c) FixRes: is a full-res tail or BN fix needed?**
- Fine-tuning at the test resolution fixes the train/test mismatch without a separate BN fix (C0198, C0199).
- E040 already ends with 5.25 full-res epochs. This **explains** why E040 worked, and it agrees with E035, where BN recalibration was a null result. Don't spend runs on a BN fix.
- The open question is how short the 32 px tail can be. E043/E044 suggest it is about 4.5–5 epochs.

**(d) Critical batch size and batch ramps for Muon.** No evidence in these cards. H1.2.3 stays open, with E041 (bs 1536 saved ~5% but left a thin margin) as the only local data point.

**C0192 and C0197** are throughput and memory facts only; they don't bear on accuracy.

## Claims
- C0184 (conditional; H2.3, H1.2.3): Multigrid scales batch size and lr up at low resolution, but in 100+ epoch video training. Test bs ~1536 during the 24 px phase only, then bs 1024 at 32 px.
- C0187 (conditional; H1.2.3): Linear lr scaling applies to the SGD group (×1.5 at bs 1536). Muon updates are normalized, and E046 (Muon lr 0.20) did not recover E041's loss, so leave the Muon lr alone.
- C0188 (conditional; H1.2.3): Large batches need warmup. Keep the warmup fraction if the batch is raised during low-res.
- C0186 (untested; H2.3, H1.2.3): Changing two size axes at once was worse than nesting them. Stagger the resolution and batch switches, and ablate each change separately.
- C0195 (contradicts; H2.3.2): EfficientNetV2 runs low-res through peak lr with one global schedule and no adjustment at switches. E043's loss is better read as fewer full-res epochs at a steep knee.
- C0193 (untested; H1.8, H1.8.2, H2.3): Small images want weaker regularization. Drop translate-2 and jitter during the 24 px phase.
- C0198 (explains; H2.3.2, H1.6.5): Fine-tuning at test resolution fixes the train/test mismatch without a BN fix. E040's long 32 px tail already covers this, consistent with E035's null BN recalibration.
- C0190 (conditional; H2.3.3): There is a minimum viable training resolution. Weakly consistent with the E044 28 px stage not paying; don't go below 24 px.

## Lit digest: Muon for short-budget CNN training (H1.2)

**Short answer:** none of the verified cards measures Muon in a regime like ours: a small CNN on CIFAR, trained for about 10 epochs to a fixed accuracy, scored on wall-clock time. So the literature neither supports nor refutes H1.2.1's ≥10% time gain. airbench94_muon, the source for H1.2, has no verified card here. The cards only show that Muon is plausible for convolutions and suggest how to keep its per-step cost low.

**Where the loop stands.** The 9-epoch recipe scores 0.7552 in 8.34 s (E002). The 10-epoch recipe scores 0.7584 in 9.28 s (E001). That is about 0.32 pp per epoch, below the ≥0.4 pp H1.1.3 predicted. Each epoch costs about 0.94 s. E003 reproduced E002's accuracy exactly, with about a 1% difference in time. So accuracy is deterministic given the seeds, and differences of 0.1 pp are real. To be worth it, Muon has to save at least about 1 epoch at matched accuracy, net of its Newton–Schulz (NS) cost.

**Muon on conv kernels (all conditional).**
- **C0006, C0007, C0008:** Muon-C beats both unfolded Muon and Adam. Early FID is 9.87 vs 22.26 vs 51.31. It needs 36–38% fewer FLOPs to reach final quality, and still wins under equal tuning budgets.
  - All of this is CIFAR-10 *flow matching* over 40k–400k steps, a long-horizon generative task. It does not transfer to a 9-epoch classifier.
- **C0009:** the same result on ImageNet-32, also flow matching. It is mapped to H1.3, but it really bears on H1.2.
- **C0010:** Muon-C gives the best validation accuracy for ResNet/ConvNeXt classification on ImageNet-100. The card gives no effect size and no time-to-target. It is also mapped to H1.3 by mistake.
- **Untested mechanism:** Muon-C's "operator-aligned" treatment of conv kernels is a different way to reshape the kernel than airbench's plain flattening. The tree has no node for it. Add it as an H1.2 child only if plain Muon (H1.2.1) shows a signal first.

**Keeping NS overhead down (relevant to the per-step question).**
- **C0012:** a cubic NS schedule uses 10 matmuls instead of 15 for quintic, a third less optimizer compute. This is untested here and bears on H1.2.1's net time.
- **C0013, C0014, C0015:** final quality barely depends on how exactly NS orthogonalizes. Cubic5 matches quintic within about 1e-3 loss, but the evidence is from GPT-2 and 1–4B MoE/Mamba models (conditional). Implication: use few NS steps (3–5) in bf16, and batch NS across the conv layers.

**Recommended action.** Run H1.2.1 directly as an experiment, because the literature can't settle it:
- Muon on the 4D conv filters only.
- Nesterov SGD for the BatchNorm biases, the whitening layer (frozen anyway) and the head.
- Muon lr ~0.24, momentum 0.6, 3–5 NS steps.
- Compare at **9 and 8 epochs** against E002. Try batch size 1024 first, then 2000.
- Use the deterministic baseline (E003) to treat ≥0.1 pp as real.
- Profile the optimizer step separately. If NS takes more than about 5% of step time, try cubic NS (C0012) before dropping H1.2.

Leave the confidence in H1.2 at 0.5. These cards give no evidence for updating it.

## Claims
- C0006 (conditional; H1.2): Muon-C beats unfolded Muon and Adam early in training, but only in long-horizon CIFAR-10 flow matching. It does not transfer to a 9-epoch classifier; test H1.2.1 directly.
- C0007 (conditional; H1.2, H1.2.1): Muon-C reaches the target with 36-38% fewer FLOPs in a generative 400k-step regime. Treat this as a weak prior that Muon can save compute; it says nothing about time-to-0.753 in ~9 epochs.
- C0008 (conditional; H1.2): Muon-C still wins under equal tuning budgets, but the FID gap is small and the task is different. Do not update H1.2 confidence.
- C0009 (conditional; H1.2): Wrongly mapped to H1.3; it is about the optimizer, not label smoothing. The task is ImageNet-32 flow matching, a regime this loop does not use.
- C0010 (untested; H1.2): Muon-C beats the baselines on ImageNet-100 CNN classification, but with no effect size or time-to-target. Operator-aligned kernel reshaping is a mechanism the tree lacks; add it as an H1.2 child only if plain Muon shows a signal.
- C0011 (conditional; H1.2): General motivation for Muon (approximately orthogonal updates) with no measured effect size. Gives no help in sizing the epoch saving.
- C0012 (untested; H1.2.1): Cubic NS needs 10 matmuls instead of 15, a third less optimizer compute. Use it if profiling shows NS costs more than about 5% of step time in the ~8 s run.
- C0013 (conditional; H1.2): How exactly NS orthogonalizes barely affects final loss (GPT-2). This justifies using few NS steps in bf16 for the CNN, but confirm accuracy at 9 epochs.
- C0014 (conditional; H1.2.1): Cubic5 matches quintic NS within about 1e-3 loss on 1-4B models. Weak support for the cheaper schedule in our small CNN.
- C0015 (conditional; H1.2.1): Cubic5 matches training quality at lower overhead in LM settings. Treat it as the fallback if Muon's step cost erases its epoch saving.

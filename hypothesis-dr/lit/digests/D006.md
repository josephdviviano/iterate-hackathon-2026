# D006: The Muon base is now the tip (E018: 7.5 epochs, 0.7596, 7.39 s), so the knee is probably around 7 epochs. Raising the Muon lr from 0.24 to 0.36 cost about 1 pp (E019), and removing lookahead cost about 1.3 pp (E020). Which Muon variants and settings improve accuracy per epoch for small CNNs on very short schedules? That covers lr and momentum (lower lr, higher momentum, or a momentum warmup), Muon weight decay, the update-scaling rule (spectral/RMS matching versus sqrt(out/in)), Muon on the head or a 2D whitening/first conv, and newer variants (NorMuon, AdaMuon, Dion, Polar Express or other NS coefficients). The answer should also say which weight-averaging or lr-tail scheme pairs best with Muon (lookahead, EMA, schedule-free, WSD cooldown). Each lever should be judged by whether it can move the 0.753 knee from about 7.5 to 6.5 epochs at under 2% extra step time.

Request R006 (auto, after E020); 80 papers found, 15 relevant, 5 read in full, 21 verified claims; cost $0.79

**Bottom line:** none of the cards covers small CNNs on short schedules. All the evidence comes from LLMs or ViTs, and most of it is about orthogonalization cost, not accuracy per epoch. The cards say nothing about Muon lr or momentum warmup, Muon weight decay at this horizon, sqrt(out/in) versus RMS-matched scaling, Muon on the head or the whitening conv, or lookahead versus EMA versus WSD. The loop has to test those levers itself. Note that the tip has already moved past the question's premise: E023 runs 7.0 epochs at 0.7568 and 6.56 s. So the gap to 6.5 epochs is small, but there is only about 0.4 pp of margin left.

**Orthogonalization quality (explains a result).** C0061 and C0062 report that exact SVD is no better than approximate Newton-Schulz (NS). What decides quality is the shape of the spectral map, not polar accuracy. This matches E017: going from NS5 to NS3 lost no accuracy. *Action:* don't spend runs on Polar Express or other NS coefficients to gain accuracy. C0063 sees a transient ~2% early loss gap between polynomials. Our whole 7-epoch run counts as "early", so coefficient choice might matter a little here. That is conditional and too small to be worth a run.

**Turbo-Muon / AOL (conditional, speed only).** C0054, C0056 and C0058: AOL preconditioning lets you drop one NS iteration at no quality cost. But the gains were measured on 4096² matrices and grow with matrix size. Our flattened filters are small (128×1152 up to 576×5184), and E023 already batches them. *Action:* try NS3→NS2 with AOL only if profiling shows Muon still takes more than ~3% of step time. That would be an H2.4 systems run.

**Aurora (mechanism doesn't apply).** C0064 and C0065 fix row-norm non-uniformity on *tall* matrices. Our flattened conv filters are *wide* (out < in·k²), so the orthogonalized rows already have uniform norm. *Action:* skip it.

**PolarAdamW / per-neuron adaptive normalization (untested, best candidate).** C0068 reports +1.93 pp over Muon on 100-class ImageNet subsets, and C0069 shows it still holds at 300 epochs. The result is on DeiT-Tiny, a transformer, and C0071 also scopes it to standard transformers. Adding a cheap per-output-channel second-moment normalization after NS, in the style of NorMuon, is an elementwise cost of under 1%. The tree has no node for this. *Action:* add H1.2.3: "Muon + per-row adaptive normalization raises accuracy by ≥0.3 pp at 7 epochs". If it holds, test it at 6.5 epochs.

**Schedule-free (contradicts dropping the schedule).** C0072 and C0074: schedule-free NorMuon nearly matches a scheduled spectral optimizer but still loses by 0.03 nats. E020 showed that Muon still needs averaging (−1.3 pp without lookahead). *Action:* keep the scheduled lr plus lookahead. Put averaging and tail tuning (H1.6.1, H1.6.2) on the Muon base instead of trying schedule-free. C0073 shows weight decay at the fast iterate matters for long-horizon stability. That is conditional, irrelevant at 7 epochs, and doesn't justify a Muon weight-decay run.

**Gap:** E019 showed lr is sensitive in the upward direction. The planned 0.16 and lower-lr/higher-momentum probes are not backed by any card, so treat them as exploratory.

## Claims
- C0061 (explains; H1.2.1): Exact polar factor gives no quality gain; consistent with E017 NS5->NS3 being accuracy-neutral. Don't spend runs on NS coefficients for accuracy.
- C0062 (explains; H1.2.1): Quality is set by the spectral-map shape, not polar accuracy; deprioritize Polar Express and other coefficient variants.
- C0063 (conditional; H1.2.1): Only a transient ~2% early-phase gap between polynomials; our 7-epoch run is all 'early', but the effect is too small to justify a run.
- C0054 (conditional; H2.4): AOL preconditioning allows dropping one NS iteration; possible NS3->NS2 time cut, only if Muon is still >3% of step time after E023.
- C0058 (conditional; H2.4): AOL gains grow with matrix size; our filter matrices are small, so expect little benefit.
- C0064 (untested; H1.2): Row-norm issue is for tall matrices; flattened conv filters here are wide, so Aurora's fix does not apply. Skip.
- C0068 (untested; H1.2): Polar + coordinatewise adaptive normalization gives +1.9 pp over Muon on 100-class ViT; add an H1.2.3 node: NorMuon-style per-row normalization at 7 epochs, then 6.5.
- C0069 (conditional; H1.2): Advantage persists at long schedules on transformers; transfer to short-schedule CNNs is unverified.
- C0074 (contradicts; H1.6): Scheduled spectral optimizer beats schedule-free; keep scheduled lr plus lookahead and do averaging/tail tuning (H1.6.1/H1.6.2) on the Muon base.
- C0073 (conditional; H1.2): Weight decay at the fast iterate matters for long-horizon stability; not relevant at 7 epochs, so no Muon-wd run on this basis.

## Digest for H1.2.3: bs 1024 → 1536–2048 on the Muon tip

**Bottom line:** the literature supports the direction but gives no numbers for this regime. No card puts the critical batch size for a small CIFAR conv net at ~6.5 epochs. None measures Muon against SGD on vision at fixed epochs, and none covers averaging horizons. Only an experiment will settle H1.2.3.

**What the cards say**
- **C0112 / C0113** (Muon theory; L-smooth loss, bounded variance). Muon has a finite critical batch B\*. The number of steps needed falls with B, but convexly, so the returns shrink. *Conditional.* The tip runs about 49 steps per epoch. At 1536 that drops to about 33, roughly 213 steps in total. If 1024 is already near B\*, accuracy at a fixed epoch count will fall.
- **C0114–C0118** (generic optimizers). Steps needed are a rational function of B, flat above B\*, and B\* depends on the optimizer. Momentum and Adam-type methods reach a larger B\* than plain SGD. *Conditional.* This is a weak prior that Muon, a momentum method, tolerates 1536 better than the old SGD base did.
- **C0119** (Muon has a higher B\* than SGD). Shown only for linear associative memory in a one-step analysis. *Conditional*, toy regime. Do not use it to size the effect.
- **C0121 / C0122.** Muon's advantage over SGD shows up early in training. Asymptotic speed is similar. *Explains* why Muon saved about 2.5 epochs in short runs (E014, E016–E022). It also implies the gain will not grow if the schedule gets longer.
- **C0123 / C0124.** Under a fixed epoch budget, larger batches need the lr and warmup re-tuned, because there are fewer updates. *Conditional.* E031 (removing Muon warmup) came out null at bs 1024. That does not carry over to 1536. Keep the warmup fraction and scale the SGD-group lr.

**Break-even arithmetic (from this loop's own numbers).** The tip at bs 1024 is 5.89 s, 0.7551, so it has 0.21 pp of margin. Each extra epoch costs about 15% of time and gains about 1.14 pp. The Muon step and lookahead take about 1.4–1.5 ms per step out of about 18 ms. Removing a third of the steps saves roughly 2.5–3%. Larger GEMMs might add 1–2%, which is the only way to reach the 4% target. Any accuracy loss above about 0.2 pp breaks the gate. Recovering a 0.3 pp loss with extra epochs costs about 4% time, which cancels the saving.

**What to do**
1. **Time it first (not an experiment).** In drafts/, time one bs-1536 epoch against one bs-1024 epoch with untimed synthetic data. If the per-epoch saving is under 4%, retire H1.2.3 without spending an experiment on it.
2. **If the saving is there, make one run at bs 1536 with the tip's epoch count.** Keep the Muon lr, since Muon's updates are normalized. Scale the SGD-group lr and weight decay by about √1.5 to 1.5. Keep the warmup as a fraction of steps.
   - The lookahead horizon is set per step, so it shortens in samples at the larger batch. Rescale it to hold the horizon fixed in samples: sync every ~3–4 steps, or decay 0.97^(5·1.5) per sync. No card covers this. It is a judgement call, so flag it in the pre-registration.
   - Pre-register the outcomes: a drop of more than 0.25 pp refutes H1.2.3, and a drop of 0.2 pp or less with a time cut of 4% or more supports it.
3. **Skip bs 2048.** C0113's convexity implies 2048 would cost even more accuracy per step saved.

C0120, C0125 and C0126 are not relevant here.

## Claims
- C0112 (conditional; H1.2.3): Muon has a finite critical batch; going above it costs accuracy at fixed epochs. Its location for this net is unknown, so test 1536 rather than 2048.
- C0113 (conditional; H1.2.3): Steps needed fall convexly as B grows, with shrinking returns. Expect some per-epoch accuracy loss when going from about 319 to about 213 steps. Pre-register a drop above 0.25 pp as refuting.
- C0118 (conditional; H1.2.3, H1.2): Momentum-type optimizers reach a larger critical batch than SGD. This is a weak prior that the Muon base tolerates bs 1536 better than the SGD base would.
- C0119 (conditional; H1.2.3): Muon has a higher critical batch than SGD, but only in a toy linear associative-memory setting. Treat it as directional only, not as a size estimate.
- C0121 (explains; H1.2, H1.2.1): Muon's advantage is front-loaded, which fits the ~2.5-epoch saving seen in short runs (E014-E022). The gain will not grow with a longer schedule.
- C0122 (explains; H1.2): Asymptotic speed is the same as SGD, so Muon's gain lives in the short-horizon regime. This supports staying near ~6.5 epochs.
- C0124 (conditional; H1.2.3, H1.2): Under fixed epochs, a larger batch makes lr and warmup more critical. Keep the warmup fraction, scale the SGD-group lr, and do not carry over E031's null result to bs 1536.
- C0123 (conditional; H1.2.3): Large-batch runs degrade without re-tuned schedules. Scale the SGD-group lr and wd with batch size.

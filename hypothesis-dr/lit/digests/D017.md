# D017: The resolution curriculum is now the main source of savings. E040 (24→32 px) cut ~3%, E047 (20 px first stage) ~3.5%, and E048 (24→28→32) brought the tip to 5.00 s. Two attempts failed: moving low-res steps later in training (E043) and adding a 28 px stage without the head-lr margin (E044). Both lost ~0.3-0.4 pp. Every other recent lever has been flat: head lr x3 (E049), Muon lr at bs 1536 (E046), and brightness/contrast jitter (E042). The question is how to schedule image resolution in a short, from-scratch CNN run so that more of the 3.75 full-res epochs can move to cheaper low-res steps without losing accuracy. Four sub-questions: (1) Should regularization or augmentation strength (translate, jitter, label smoothing) scale with resolution, as in EfficientNetV2-style progressive learning? (2) How should batch size and lr change across resolution stages? (3) How short can the final full-res stage be while still correcting the train/test resolution discrepancy (FixRes), for example with BN re-adaptation on training data or a short full-res tail? (4) Do smooth resolution ramps beat step schedules?

Request R017 (auto, after E049); 80 papers found, 2 relevant, 2 read in full, 9 verified claims; cost $0.79

## Resolution scheduling: what the cards say

**Coverage.** All nine cards come from ImageNet or video super-resolution runs with schedules of hundreds of epochs. They speak to sub-question (1) and partly to (3). None of them answers (2) how batch size and lr should change per stage, or (4) smooth ramps versus steps. No card says how short the final full-res tail can be.

**(1) Regularization should scale with resolution. This is the most useful lead.**
- **C0228 / C0229 (conditional):** in EfficientNetV2, weak augmentation suits small images and strong augmentation suits large ones. At size 128, RandAug magnitude 5 is best. At size 300, magnitude 15 beats 5 by 0.7 pp. Training small-and-weak early, then large-and-strong, removed the accuracy drop seen in earlier progressive-resizing work. The regime is 350 epochs with large RandAug magnitudes. Here the run is 6.75 epochs with mild augmentation.
- **C0231 (untested mechanism):** earlier progressive resizing lost accuracy because it did not adjust regularization. This is a candidate explanation for E043 (more low-res steps, -0.48 pp) and E044 (28 px stage, -0.32 pp). In both, translate-2 and jitter ran at full strength in every stage. H2.3 does not yet contain this mechanism.
- **Action:** add an H2.3 child. Turn jitter off (and possibly translate) in the 20/28 px stages and keep both at 32 px. This costs no time, and E042 showed jitter is roughly neutral overall. If accuracy holds or rises, rerun the shortened 32 px tail (for example 3.0 epochs) with this coupling.
- **C0232 (conditional, low value):** the card scales dropout with resolution. LS is the analogue here, and it was flat from 0.1 to 0.3 (E004, E036), so LS is not the axis to scale.

**(3) FixRes and BN.**
- **C0233 (explains):** BN weights carry across resolution stages without re-initialization. That matches the clean keeps in E040, E047 and E048.
- **C0230 (conditional):** FixRes gains come from the object-size mismatch that RandomResizedCrop creates on ImageNet. Here whole images are downsampled, so the mismatch has a different form. Taken together with E035, where BN recalibration added nothing at 32 px, re-adapting BN is unlikely to rescue a shorter tail. **Action:** treat BN re-adaptation as low priority. Test tail length directly instead.

**(2) and (4) Batch size, lr and ramp shape.**
- **C0234 (conditional):** EfficientNetV2 interpolates regularization linearly across 4 discrete stages. That supports step schedules with interpolated settings, not continuous ramps.
- **C0236 (conditional):** large-batch runs need warmup. The recipe already has warmup, and E031 showed Muon does not need it. Nothing new here.
- **C0235 (untested, weak):** the video work warns against changing two curriculum axes at once. If you try larger batches at low resolution, which no card covers, do not move the batch size and the resolution boundary in the same experiment.

**Bottom line:** the literature's one concrete lever is to weaken augmentation in the low-res stages. Test that before pushing the 32 px boundary later again.

## Claims
- C0228 (conditional; H2.3, H1.8, H1.8.2): Augmentation strength should rise with resolution (ImageNet, long schedules). Try jitter and translate off in the low-res stages and on at 32 px.
- C0229 (conditional; H2.3, H2.3.3): Small-and-weak early, then large-and-strong, removed the progressive-resizing accuracy drop. Basis for retrying a shorter 32 px tail once regularization is coupled to resolution.
- C0231 (untested; H2.3, H2.3.2, H2.3.3): Candidate mechanism for the E043/E044 losses: resolution changed without adjusting regularization. Add as a new H2.3 child and test with stage-dependent augmentation.
- C0232 (conditional; H1.7.2): Dropout scaled with resolution. The analogue here is LS, which is flat from 0.1 to 0.3 (E004, E036), so deprioritize resolution-scaled LS.
- C0233 (explains; H2.3): BN weights transfer across resolution stages, which matches the clean E040/E047/E048 keeps without re-initialization.
- C0230 (conditional; H2.3, H1.6.5): The FixRes benefit relies on the RandomResizedCrop object-size mismatch, which whole-image downsampling does not reproduce. With E035 flat, BN re-adaptation is low priority; test tail length directly.
- C0234 (conditional; H2.3): Linearly interpolated settings across discrete stages work. Mild support for step schedules with interpolated augmentation, not evidence for continuous ramps.
- C0236 (conditional; H1.2.3): Large batches need warmup. Already in the recipe, and Muon did not need it (E031). Nothing new.
- C0235 (untested; H2.3, H1.2.3): Weak analogy: do not change two curriculum axes at once. If larger batches are tried in low-res stages, test separately from boundary moves.

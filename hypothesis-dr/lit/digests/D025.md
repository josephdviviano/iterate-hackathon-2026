# D025: E058 was a surprise. Its prior was 0.10, yet freezing block 1 for the last 20% of samples (FreezeOut-style) cut time by 4.8%, bringing the tip from 4.93 s to 4.70 s, and accuracy was 0.7537. That is only 0.07 pp above the 0.753 gate, and the accuracy delta of about -0.08 pp is within re-draw noise. This opens an axis the tree never mapped: progressive layer freezing as a compute schedule. What does the literature say about how to schedule layer freezing in short-horizon CNN training? Specifically: (a) how early, and in what order, early layers or blocks can be frozen before accuracy drops, and whether freeze timing should follow the lr schedule or a per-layer convergence signal (gradient norm, weight change, representation similarity / SVCCA); (b) whether a second block (block 2) or partial freezing (the BN affine params or the first conv only) can be frozen too; (c) how freezing interacts with weight averaging (lookahead/EMA), BatchNorm running statistics, and normalized or orthogonalized optimizers such as Muon; (d) whether the freed compute is better spent on extra late-phase steps, to win back the accuracy margin at equal time.

## Layer freezing as a compute schedule: what the cards say

**Why E058 worked.** Early layers converge first and need the least late fine-tuning (C0237, C0246). This *explains* E058: block 1 was frozen for the last 20% of samples and accuracy moved by only about -0.08 pp. FreezeOut also freezes each layer's BN together with its conv (C0249), which is what E058 did with the block-1 BN affine params. That confirms the E058 design and calls for no change.

**(a) Order and timing.** FreezeOut freezes layers in order, early to late (C0239). It ties freeze points to the lr schedule: each layer's lr is cosine-annealed to zero at a pre-set fraction of training, and the layer is then frozen (C0238). E058 instead froze abruptly at 0.8 while the Muon lr was still above its 0.07x floor. A per-layer anneal for block 1 alone is *untested* here, so it is a candidate refinement. It is not the same as the global decay-to-0 that hurt in E025. Convergence signals such as CKA or weight change are noisy and non-monotonic, so they need long histories (C0247). A ~200-step run cannot afford that, so use fixed sample-indexed freeze points. The CKA-guided 44-48% FLOP savings (C0248) come from fine-tuning *pretrained* nets for 10 epochs. That is *conditional* on a regime this loop cannot use, so do not take the magnitude as a target.

**(b) Block 2 and partial freezing.** FreezeOut found no speedup on VGG, which has no skip connections, and a 3% error cost on DenseNet at a 20% speedup (C0240, C0241). That is why H2.4.7's prior was 0.10. E058 now *contradicts* it for this skip-free net. Those VGG results came from one strategy on 100-epoch runs, so discount them. Still, with no skip connections, freezing block 2 cuts off all gradient flow to block 1. Block 2 can therefore only be frozen after block 1 (the forward order, also C0251), and never earlier. The BNN ordering results (C0250-C0252) are *conditional* on binary nets and weak evidence here. For a cheap partial step, FreezeOut also drops the BN statistics computation for frozen layers by running them in inference mode (C0242). E058 kept block 1's BN in train mode. Running it in eval mode is *untested* and would save a little more time.

**(c) Lookahead/EMA, BN statistics and Muon.** No card covers any of these interactions. This is a literature gap: rely on the loop's own experiments.

**(d) Reinvesting the freed compute.** No card covers this either. The loop's own numbers decide it. E058 cost about 0.017 pp per 1% of time, while extra epochs buy about 0.08 pp per 1% (H5). Freezing is therefore about 5x cheaper than epochs. Two moves follow:
- Pull the block-1 freeze earlier, to 0.7 then 0.6.
- Then add a block-2 freeze over about the last 10%.

The margin to the gate is now only 0.07 pp. So pair any aggressive freeze with +0.25 epoch, and check it on extra seeds offline in drafts/ (H4.1.2) before keeping it.

## Claims
- C0237 (explains; H2.4.7): Early layers converge first, which explains why freezing block 1 for the last 20% cost only about 0.08 pp in E058.
- C0246 (explains; H2.4.7): Early layers converging faster supports freezing block 1 first and suggests moving the freeze point earlier.
- C0249 (explains; H2.4.7): Freezing BN together with its conv matches E058's design, so no change is needed.
- C0238 (untested; H2.4.7, H1.6): Anneal block 1's lr to zero before freezing it, instead of freezing abruptly. Test this as a refinement of E058.
- C0239 (conditional; H2.4.7): Freeze in order, early to late: block 2 only after block 1 has been frozen.
- C0240 (contradicts; H2.4.7): FreezeOut predicted no gain for skip-free nets, but E058 gained 4.8%. Lower the weight of this prior when judging block-2 freezing.
- C0241 (contradicts; H2.4.7): The VGG no-speedup result does not transfer to this setting. Its warning about cut gradient paths still means block 2 must not be frozen before block 1.
- C0242 (untested; H2.4.7, H2.4): Run the frozen block 1 with BN in eval mode to skip the statistics computation, for a small extra time saving.
- C0247 (conditional; H2.4.7): Convergence signals are too noisy for ~200 steps, so use fixed sample-indexed freeze points.
- C0248 (conditional; H2.4.7): The 44-48% savings come from fine-tuning pretrained nets. Do not treat that magnitude as a target here.
- C0251 (conditional; H2.4.7): BNN evidence that reverse-order freezing collapses accuracy. Weak support for the forward order only.

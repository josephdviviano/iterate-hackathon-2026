# D005: E014 was surprising. Muon on the conv filters, with lookahead kept, reached 0.768 at 9 epochs, +1.3 pp over the tip and far above the predicted range. But the run took 9.18 s against the tip's 8.09 s, so the optimizer step costs about 13% more time. That much accuracy headroom is worth roughly 2 epochs, which makes H1.2 the most decision-relevant open branch. The question: how can Muon's per-step cost be cut, and how should its hyperparameters be retuned so that a Muon recipe clears 0.753 at about 6.5-7.5 epochs in less time than the tip? Specifically: (a) cheaper orthogonalization, via fewer Newton-Schulz steps (3 vs 5), tuned quintic coefficients, batching or stacking same-shape filters into one batched matmul, lower precision, or orthogonalizing less often than every step; (b) how Muon's lr, momentum, Nesterov use, and the warmup/decay schedule should change when the run is shortened to fewer epochs or steps; (c) whether Muon interacts with lookahead or EMA weight averaging, and with batch size, in short training runs.

## Muon cost and retuning: what the literature says

**Where things stand.** The loop has already answered part of the question. Moving from 5 to 3 Newton-Schulz (NS) steps (E017) kept accuracy and saved about 0.23 s at 8 epochs. That puts the cost of the 3 remaining steps at roughly 0.3 s, about 4% of the new tip (E018: 7.39 s, 0.7596). E018 also clears the gate with about 0.65 pp to spare at 7.5 epochs, and one epoch is worth about 0.3–0.8 pp. **Cutting epochs (7.0, then 6.5) is still worth more than any change to orthogonalization**, which can save at most about 4%.

**(a) Cheaper orthogonalization**
- **C0047** (explains): standard Muon uses 5 NS steps. E017 shows 3 is enough here, as in airbench94_muon.
- **C0036, C0039, C0040, C0041** (conditional; tested on NanoGPT and synthetic matrices): tuned Chebyshev-type (CANS) coefficients reach the same orthogonalization quality with fewer matmuls. A larger polynomial derivative at zero also helps convergence. Next: try **2 steps with CANS-style coefficients** against the 3-step tip. Expect at most about 1.5% time saved. That is near the jitter, so it needs a paired rerun.
- **C0031, C0034** (untested mechanism): batching same-shape matrices across layers into one batched matmul (bmm) leaves the optimizer state and dynamics unchanged. Here each block has two convs of the same shape: 128×1152, 384×3456 and 576×5184. Stacking each pair is math-identical apart from re-draw noise (H4.1). Measure the optimizer step time per step first. H2.2 found little launch overhead, so the gain may be small.
- **C0029, C0030, C0033, C0032** (conditional; transformer setting): tiled NS trades spectral coupling for cost, with a "full early, tiled late" schedule. This is a plausible way to orthogonalize less in late steps, but there is no evidence for convs or short runs.
- **C0051** (explains): bf16 is adequate for this spectral computation, consistent with E014's bf16 NS working.
- **C0050, C0052, C0053** (conditional; LLM-scale): adaptive spectral bounds cut 2–3 NS steps for a target error. The machinery is too heavy for a 7 s run and low priority.
- **C0042–C0046** (IFNSO): evidence is weak (MNIST, batch size 32). Skip.

**(b) lr, momentum and schedule under fewer steps.** No card addresses this. E019 already refuted raising the Muon lr to 0.36 (−1 pp). The pre-registered follow-up is 0.16–0.20, and Muon momentum (0.6) is still untuned.

**(c) Interaction with lookahead/EMA and batch size.** No card addresses short-run averaging. **C0049** (untested here) is theory showing Muon can fail to converge for nearly all mini-batch sizes on simple problems. It does not bear on 7-epoch runs, so leave batch size at 1024.

**Recommended next steps, in order:**
1. 7.0 epochs on E018.
2. Muon lr 0.18.
3. Batch the same-shape NS pairs.
4. 2-step CANS coefficients.

## Claims
- C0047 (explains; H1.2.1): Standard Muon uses 5 NS steps. E017 shows 3 is enough here; the remaining NS cost is ~4% of time.
- C0036 (conditional; H1.2.1): CANS coefficients beat standard NS at the same matmul count (NanoGPT). Try 2 tuned steps against 3.
- C0039 (conditional; H1.2.1): Tuned coefficients need fewer matmuls. Supports trying a 2-step NS.
- C0040 (conditional; H1.2.1): A higher derivative at zero at no extra matmuls. Pick coefficients by this criterion.
- C0041 (conditional; H1.2.1): Same as C0040; coefficient source in Appendix J.
- C0031 (untested; H1.2.1, H2): Batch the same-shape conv filter pairs per block into one bmm for NS. Measure optimizer step time first.
- C0034 (untested; H1.2.1): Batching leaves momentum/wd dynamics unchanged, so it is a pure systems change (subject to re-draw noise).
- C0029 (conditional; H1.2.1): Tiled NS cuts cost; transformer-only evidence. Low priority.
- C0030 (conditional; H1.2.1): SRAM-resident small-tile kernels; would need a custom kernel. Low priority.
- C0033 (conditional; H1.2): Tile size trades coupling vs cost; no conv evidence.
- C0032 (conditional; H1.2): Full-early, cheap-late orthogonalization schedule; a possible way to orthogonalize less often late in training.
- C0051 (explains; H1.2.1): bf16 is adequate for the spectral computation, consistent with bf16 NS working in E014/E016.
- C0050 (conditional; H1.2.1): Wider spectral intervals need more steps; adaptive bounds are too heavy here.
- C0052 (conditional; H1.2.1): Spectral estimation saves 2-3 steps at LLM scale. Low priority.
- C0053 (conditional; H1.2): LLM pretraining gains; not transferable evidence.
- C0042 (conditional; H1.2.1): IFNSO; weak evidence. Skip.
- C0044 (conditional; H1.2.1): MNIST bs32 only. Skip.
- C0049 (untested; H1.2): Theoretical batch-size non-convergence; irrelevant at 7 epochs. Keep bs 1024.

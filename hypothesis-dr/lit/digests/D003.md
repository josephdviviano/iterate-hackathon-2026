## Muon on conv filters in a short CIFAR run: what the cards show

**Short answer: the cards don't settle it.** None of them compares Muon with tuned Nesterov SGD on CIFAR classification in under 10 epochs, on CIFAR-10 or CIFAR-100. None reports lr, momentum, Newton-Schulz (NS) step count or batch size for a small CNN. The settings in H1.2.1 (lr ~0.24, momentum 0.6, Nesterov, bs ~2000, linear decay) still come only from the `airbench94_muon.py` source. **H1.2 stays at about 0.45–0.5. Nothing here supports or refutes it.**

**What the cards do give:**
- **Conv kernels, other tasks (conditional).** These results are generative or long-horizon, and the baselines are other Muon variants or Adam, not tuned SGD:
  - **C0016:** on CIFAR-10 flow matching at 40k iterations, FID was 9.87 for Muon-C vs 22.26 for flattened ("unfolded") Muon vs 51.31 for Adam.
  - **C0018:** the same pattern on ImageNet-32.
  - **C0019:** Muon-C gave the best ImageNet-100 accuracy on ResNet-34/50 and ConvNeXtV2-T.
  - None of this transfers directly to a 9-epoch, 0.753 knee.
- **A mechanism the tree lacks (untested).**
  - **C0016/C0017:** how you split a 4D conv kernel into matrices before orthogonalizing matters. Splitting by frequency (Muon-C) beat both the flattened version and splitting by spatial offset (Muon-S). It also had the stronger *early* trajectory, which is the part that counts in a 9-epoch run.
  - airbench94_muon flattens kernels. If plain Muon fails, try a frequency-wise variant before closing H1.2.
- **NS quality depends on each matrix (untested).**
  - **C0024, C0026, C0027:** finite-step NS quality depends on each matrix's stable rank and condition number. A single fixed step count under- or over-orthogonalizes some layers.
  - Here the shapes differ a lot: the frozen whitening layer, which must stay excluded, the 128-wide stem and the 576×5184 last block.
  - If Muon underperforms, first check NS steps per layer (about 3 vs 5), then the lr.
- **Overhead (conditional, LLM only).**
  - **C0020/C0021:** Newton-Muon used 6% fewer steps to the target loss but saved only about 4% of wall-clock time. Extra optimizer math eats roughly a third of the step saving there.
  - Expect the same here. Muon must save clearly more than 10% of steps to meet H1.2.1's ≥10% time target.
- **Not relevant to this question:** C0022 (theory only), C0023 (no effect size), C0025/C0028 (Muon in LLM pretraining).

**How this bears on the loop's results:**
- **E005** refuted H2.2.1: the fused, fully compiled step cut 0.001 s and lost 0.1 pp. When adding Muon, leave the existing SGD path for BatchNorm biases (lr ×64) and the head untouched.
- Put only the 3×3 conv filters on Muon, in a separately compiled NS function warmed up in build().

**Next step:** run H1.2.1 directly.
1. Use the airbench94_muon settings at 9 epochs, comparing bs 2000 against the current bs 1024, and log the share of step time spent in NS.
2. Then test 8 epochs. The E002–E006 spread suggests the knee moves about 0.4 pp per epoch.

A Muon run that reaches ≥0.753 at 8 epochs with NS overhead under 5% would support H1.2.1.

## Claims
- C0016 (untested; H1.2, H1.2.1): Points to a mechanism the tree lacks: how a 4D conv kernel is split into matrices (frequency-wise Muon-C vs flattened) strongly affects Muon on conv nets. If plain flattened Muon fails at 8-9 epochs, add a frequency-wise variant under H1.2 before refuting it.
- C0017 (untested; H1.2, H1.2.1): Muon-C's stronger early trajectory is what counts in a 9-epoch run. This supports trying it as the H1.2 follow-up variant.
- C0018 (conditional; H1.2): Generative ImageNet-32 result, compared against unfolded Muon rather than SGD. Not direct evidence for the short CIFAR-100 classification regime; do not change confidence.
- C0019 (conditional; H1.2, H1.2.1): Classification gain on ImageNet-100 ResNets under long training. Suggests Muon-type optimizers help conv classifiers, but whether that holds at under 10 epochs against tuned SGD is untested; check it with the H1.2.1 run.
- C0020 (conditional; H1.2.1): LLM-only result: 6% fewer steps. Use as a reference point; the step saving Muon needs here must be well above the 10% time target.
- C0021 (conditional; H1.2.1): 6% fewer steps gave only about 4% wall-clock saving, so optimizer overhead eats about a third of the step gain. Log the share of step time spent in NS in the H1.2.1 experiment.
- C0024 (untested; H1.2): NS quality depends on each matrix's spectrum. Layer shapes here are very heterogeneous, so tune NS steps per layer (3 vs 5) if Muon underperforms.
- C0026 (untested; H1.2): Orthogonalization difficulty differs per matrix. Keep the frozen whitening layer, biases and head off Muon, and route only the 3x3 conv filters to it.
- C0027 (untested; H1.2.1): A single uniform NS schedule leaves some layers under- or over-orthogonalized. Candidate secondary ablation (per-layer NS steps) after the base Muon run.

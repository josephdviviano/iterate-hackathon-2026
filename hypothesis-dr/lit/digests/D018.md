# Digest: what sets the lowest useful early resolution in a resolution curriculum

**No verified literature claims were found for this question.** No card was checked against a paper, so nothing below comes from the literature. In particular, I can't cite EfficientNetV2 progressive learning, FixRes, or MosaicML progressive resizing as support. What follows is reasoning from this loop's own results. It is not literature evidence and should be weighted that way.

## What the loop's data already suggests

- **The 16 px cliff is probably architectural.** In E050 (-1.4 pp), the 16 px pre-print sizes were whitening 15, then 7, then 3, and the final map was **1x1**. Block 3 ran its 3x3 convs on a 3x3 map that is mostly zero padding, and the global pool had nothing left to pool. At 20 px (E047) the final map was still 2x2. The jump from a 0.18 pp loss (24->20) to a 1.4 pp loss (20->16) fits the point where block 3 loses its spatial structure better than it fits a smooth loss of image information. This mechanism isn't in the tree. It belongs under H2.3, as "the minimum useful stage resolution is set by the final map staying >=2x2."
- **A train-test BN mismatch is unlikely to be the issue.** The last 3.75 epochs (E048) run at 32 px, so the BN running statistics are rewritten at the eval resolution before the end. E035 (BN recalibration) also gained nothing. A FixRes-style BN or fine-tune fix has no visible failure to repair here, so I'd give it low priority.
- **Augmentation scale relative to image size.** Translate-2 is applied *before* downsampling (E047/E050), so in source pixels it shrinks along with the image. It is not stronger at low resolution. Only the 2x2 whitening patches cover a larger fraction of the image as resolution drops. The EfficientNetV2-style "weaker aug at low res" argument is therefore weaker here than it looks. It is untested (H1.8.2 is the nearest node).
- **The boundary result is consistent.** E043 (low-res pushed into the peak-lr phase) hurt and E044/E048 (28 px middle stage) helped. Together they say that steps after warmup need nearly full resolution. Neither a larger batch nor a different lr in the low-res stage has been tested.

## Suggested next steps (from the loop's own reasoning, not the literature)

1. **16 px with one fewer downsampling.** Run the 16 px stage with one pool skipped in the 16 px graph only, so the final map stays >=2x2. FLOPs then sit between the 16 px and 20 px graphs. If this recovers most of the 1.4 pp, it confirms the spatial-collapse story.
2. **Larger batch in the warmup stage.** Use something like bs 2048 at 20 px for epochs 0-1.5. That stage has already been shown to be nearly free in accuracy, so cutting its step count is the cheaper lever. Moving the boundary later is not, given E043.
3. **Skip a FixRes/BN fine-tune unless new evidence appears.** E035 already covers this regime.

## Post-mortem
<!-- For failures and missed predictions: what you predicted, what happened, the root cause,
what the world model got wrong, the lesson. -->
Predicted [0.748, 0.762] (point -0.1 pp); got 0.7426 (-1.2 pp). With per-row update norms matched, the difference is the
geometry: frequency-wise orthogonalisation gives every one of the 9 DCT frequencies an equal share of the update
(all singular values ~1 per frequency). Flattened Muon instead lets the update concentrate on whichever spatial
frequencies carry the gradient, which for 3x3 kernels is mostly the low/DC components. Equalising frequencies
pumps high-frequency (checkerboard-like) components into the filters, roughly 8/9 of the update in directions the
gradient barely asked for. The world model took C0016/C0017 (generative, long horizon, unmatched norms) as weakly
transferable; the mechanism actually works against a short classification run. Lesson: an orthogonaliser whitens the
spectrum of whatever matrix it is given. Choose the matrix so that its "directions" match the ones that should get
equal weight; frequency slices of 3x3 kernels should not.

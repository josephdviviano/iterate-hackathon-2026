# E065 · I083 · H1.9

H1.9 (open, c=0.30): How strongly Muon orthogonalizes should be scheduled over training. Scale the momentum matrix fed to NS3 to a Frobenius norm below 1. Three quintic iterations then saturate only the large singular directions, which gives smaller, less whitened updates late in training. This damps late noise without removing the tail lr, which decay-to-0 did and which hurt (E025). Hiverge does this on its CIFAR-10 Muon record.
H1 (open, c=0.50): Accuracy per step is set by the structure of the update. Only two levers have moved the 3-seed mean by >=0.5 pp: Muon on the conv filters (+1.3 pp at 9 epochs, E014; 9 SGD epochs -> ~6.5 Muon epochs) and lookahead (~0.9-1 pp, E008/E020). Since then, every scalar re-tune has moved the mean by <=0.18 pp, which is within the ~0.12-0.15 pp paired re-draw sd (H4.1). These include Muon lr and momentum (E024/E026/E046), Muon warmup and floor (E031/E033), head lr x2/x3 (E045/E049), LS (E036), jitter (E042) and lookahead decay (E028). The cuts from 5.89 s to 5.00 s (E040-E048) came from making steps cheaper or fewer: the resolution curriculum and bs 1536 (N1, H2.3, H1.2.3). They did not come from a better update. The update-side levers left are structural: the head's update geometry, per-row normalization after NS (NorMuon), scheduling how strongly NS orthogonalizes (H1.9), and a batch-size schedule. Target: <=4.7 s.
Idea: RMS-matched test of scheduled orthogonalization strength. This is the form requested when I054 was dropped, so that a result is not confounded with a lower effective Muon lr (H1.2.4). On the tip (E058 or its successor), make one change inside the batched compiled Muon step. Today each momentum matrix is scaled to Frobenius norm 1 before NS3. Instead, scale it to s_t, which decays linearly from 0.50 at step 0 to 0.08 at the final step (Hiverge's CIFAR-10 schedule). Run the same 3 quintic iterations, then rescale each NS output matrix so its Frobenius norm equals that of the tip's NS output for that shape group (or, more simply, sqrt(min(out,in)) times the existing sqrt(max(1,out/in)) factor). The per-step update magnitude is therefore unchanged, and only how strongly the small singular directions are flattened changes. Pass s_t as a 0-d GPU tensor updated with fill_, so nothing recompiles. Weight-norm projection, Muon lr 0.16, momentum 0.8, lookahead, the curriculum, freeze points and epochs stay unchanged. In drafts/, log the per-group update Frobenius norm and the fraction of singular values above 0.5 at steps 0, 100 and the end, against the tip, to confirm the norms match and the spectrum is less whitened late in training. Run debug_nan.py under max-autotune-no-cudagraphs and re-warm the Muon variants in build().
Expected (from the queue): Accuracy -0.35 to +0.30 pp, point 0.0 pp (~0.7537). Time +0% to +0.5% (one extra norm and scale per group). A gain >=0.2 pp supports H1.9: late, less-whitened updates help at fixed magnitude, which banks margin for the freeze ladder. Within +-0.15 pp: orthogonalization strength is not a lever at this horizon; close H1.9 and drop H1.9.1. A drop >=0.3 pp means full whitening throughout is right, consistent with E030's lesson that equalizing directions the gradient barely asked for can hurt.

## Post-mortem
<!-- For failures and missed predictions: what you predicted, what happened, the root cause,
what the world model got wrong, the lesson. -->
Predicted [0.747, 0.759] (point -0.1 pp); got -1.51 pp. I read E030 (frequency-wise split, -1.2 pp) as "the wrong
matrices were whitened" and expected a gentler reshaping to be roughly neutral. Root cause: Muon's value here IS the
whitening. With s < 1, NS3 barely lifts the small singular directions (top/median 10-30 vs 5), so the update collapses
onto the few dominant gradient directions, like normalised SGD. The world model said "NS5 vs NS3 makes no difference" (E017)
and concluded orthogonalisation precision is unimportant. That holds near full whitening, but strength below the NS3
default falls off a cliff. Lesson: the useful range of whitening strength is at or above the NS3-at-scale-1 default;
test stronger (e.g. tuned coefficients, NS4), never weaker.

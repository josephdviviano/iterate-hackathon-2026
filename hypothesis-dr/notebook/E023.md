H2.4 (open, c=0.60): Non-conv kernels that take a visible share of GPU time can be cut with no or neutral change to accuracy, mainly by doing less work. Examples are the atomic/scatter backward of adaptive pooling and the memory-bound fused BN/GELU elementwise kernels (~3.4 ms/step, ~18%). E010 is the first instance: max_pool2d replaced adaptive_max_pool2d, -2.0% time.
H2 (open, c=0.35): With the recipe and epochs held fixed, per-step GPU work can still be cut a few percent without changing accuracy. The cuts come from removing work in non-conv kernels (atomic backward, memory-bound elementwise ops) and from conv-shape inefficiencies, not from launch or optimizer overhead or algorithm selection. Total remaining headroom is ~5-10%, not 10-30%.
Idea: Systems change to the Muon optimizer step only; the recipe math stays the same. Muon currently costs 1.93 ms/step of GPU time (about 10% of the step), mostly as many small kernels: 9 per-param calls, each with momentum, norm and projection ops plus per-iteration NS elementwise work. Replace them with one torch.compile'd Muon step over all conv filters. Do the momentum and Nesterov update with torch._foreach_* ops. Stack same-shape flattened filters within each block (128x1152, 384x3456, 576x5184 pairs) and run the 3 quintic NS iterations as one bmm per shape group. Apply the sqrt(max(1,out/in)) scaling and the weight-norm projection inside the same compiled function. Pass the Muon lr as a 0-d GPU tensor so schedule changes do not recompile. Compile and warm up in build() on synthetic tensors. Run the drafts/debug_nan.py one-step finite-gradient check under max-autotune-no-cudagraphs before launching (E009 lesson). Measure the Muon step GPU time with drafts/time_muon.py before and after.
Expected (from the queue): Muon step GPU time drops from 1.93 ms to ~0.6-1.1 ms. Total time falls 3-6%, from 7.39 s to ~7.0-7.2 s (point ~7.12 s).

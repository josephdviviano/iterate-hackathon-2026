# E012 · I013 · H2.3.1

H2.3.1 (open, c=0.20): After 2 full epochs, backpropagating only the top 60% by loss of each batch (losses taken from the main net's own forward pass) reduces time by >=5% with accuracy >=0.753.
H2.3 (open, c=0.25): Training only on high-loss examples, as in Selective Backprop / airbench96_faster, cuts backward compute. With 500 images per class and ~8 epochs, the cost of the proxy or forward pass may outweigh the gain.
H2 (open, c=0.60): Per-epoch compute drives the score. About 95% of GPU time is in conv, BN and activation kernels, so cutting FLOPs or kernel cost per epoch (lower early resolution, a cheaper activation, training on fewer examples) reduces time at constant accuracy.
Idea: Start from A1a (8.5 epochs). From the start of epoch 3, for each batch of 1024: (1) run a no-grad forward through the compiled net in train mode, with BN running-stat updates disabled, and get per-example losses; (2) keep the 614 highest-loss examples (a fixed size, so the shapes stay static); (3) run the normal forward+backward and optimizer step on those 614 only. Compile and warm up the 614-shape train step in build. Keep the LR schedule tied to the step count, which does not change.
Expected (from the queue): Time -2 to -6%: each step costs about 0.93x a full step after epoch 2, and less GPU energy. Accuracy -0.3 to -1.0 pp, likely infeasible at 8.5 epochs. The value of the run is the exchange rate. If accuracy loss per % of time saved beats the epoch-cut rate (about 0.35 pp per 6%), combine it with +0.5 epochs. Otherwise H2.3 is refuted for this data size.

## Details (full outcome and post-mortem, condensed above)

### Outcome
accuracy 0.7485 (0.7461, 0.7504, 0.7490) vs 0.75493: -0.64 pp; time 7.158 s vs 7.222: -0.9%. Exchange rate
0.71 pp per 1% time -- ~9x worse than cutting epochs (0.08 pp/1%). Verdict refutes H2.3.1. Contingency "right way
but less than predicted": the expected -4.5% did not materialize; the extra 1024-example no-grad forward plus a
614-example step cost nearly as much energy as a full 1024 step (smaller GEMMs are less efficient, and the scoring
forward is ~1/3 of a step). Hard-example selection is a dead end at ~8 epochs on 50k images. Discard.

### Post-mortem
Predicted accuracy [0.740, 0.752] (hit) and time [6.75, 7.10] (miss: 7.158). Root cause of the time miss: I assumed
energy scales linearly with examples per step; 614-example steps are less efficient per example, and the scoring
forward is not free. Lesson: on a power-capped GPU, any scheme that adds a forward pass to save part of a backward
pass nets ~nothing; only removing work outright (lower resolution, fewer channels) pays.

H10.2 (open, c=0.20): The bs-1024 32 px gain is a Muon path-length effect, not an update-count effect. Muon's orthogonalized update has a size set by the lr (and the shape scale), not by the gradient. At a fixed sample-indexed lr, 1.5x more steps at 32 px means ~1.5x more total filter movement (relative to ||w||, since the filters are renormed). If so, a larger Muon lr in the 32 px phase alone should reproduce the gain at zero time cost. The SGD-era adaptive-batch literature increases the batch late (search), so a late small-batch gain fits a Muon-specific mechanism better than a generic noise one.
H10 (open, c=0.35): On the Muon tip, at fixed epochs and resolution split, adding optimizer updates raises accuracy at ~0.04-0.065 pp per 1% of time, apparently regardless of placement. Evidence: bs 1024 at 32 px (E084/E085/E095: +0.1-0.18 pp for +2.5%) and bs 1024 in all phases (E104: +0.12 off-seed for +2.8%, one process, with a scored 0.7525 that disagrees in sign). Removing steps costs accuracy (E038, E022). The rate equals what time cuts cost, so update count is not a net lever at the current margin. Open: whether the 32 px gain is Muon path length or update count (H10.2; E100/E105 inconclusive).

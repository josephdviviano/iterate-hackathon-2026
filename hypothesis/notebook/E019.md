## Details (full outcome and post-mortem, condensed above)

### Outcome
accuracy 0.76633 (0.7658, 0.7693, 0.7639) vs tip 0.75493: **+1.14 pp**; time 8.041 s vs 7.222: +11.3% (+0.82 s).
Verdict refutes H5.1.1/H5: Muon on the 9 group convs is a large sample-efficiency gain on CIFAR-100 with this recipe
(the published "Muon lost to SGD" claim does not hold here). Contingency "opposite way (>= +0.4 pp)" fired.
Discarded only because time rose; feasible with 1.33 pp of margin.
Exchange: +1.14 pp is worth ~1.2 epochs at the SGD epoch slope (~0.95 pp/epoch, E007). If Muon's slope is similar,
Muon at ~7.3 epochs would land ~0.755. Time ~ 8.04 x 7.3/8.5 = 6.9 s with the current eager Muon, and less if
the Muon step overhead (+0.82 s over 408 steps = ~2 ms/step) is cut.
Where the +0.82 s likely comes from: eager per-parameter Newton-Schulz (9 params x 3 iterations, ~25 small kernels
each -> ~225 launches/step, CPU-launch-bound while the GPU idles), fp32->bf16 casts, weight renorm per step.
Follow-ups, in order of expected value: (1) Muon at 7.0-7.5 epochs (pure epoch cut, already feasible margin);
(2) cheaper Muon step: batch the same-shape filters (conv2/conv3 of each group share shapes) into one batched NS,
torch.compile the update, or run NS less precisely (2 steps); (3) Muon + the 24 px progressive schedule (E006),
which had the best time exchange rate; (4) Muon lr/momentum retune (0.24/0.6 came from CIFAR-10 airbench94).
Measured after the run (micro-benchmarks on the same GPU, 9 filter matrices of this net):
  in-training step: SGD 17.1 ms/step, SGD+eager Muon 19.0 ms/step (+1.9 ms, +11%).
  Muon step alone, eager: GPU time 3.8 ms, CPU launch 5.3 ms (many tiny kernels, low utilization).
  torch.compile(mode="max-autotune-no-cudagraphs") of the whole Muon update (lr passed as a 0-d tensor so the
  schedule does not recompile): GPU time 1.67 ms, CPU launch 1.9 ms -> expected in-training overhead ~+0.8 ms/step
  (~+4.5%). Eager with 2 NS steps instead of 3: GPU 2.8 ms. reduce-overhead (CUDA graphs) gave no benefit (4.0 ms).

### Post-mortem
Predicted accuracy [0.744, 0.758] -> miss high (0.7663); time [7.22, 7.55] -> miss high (8.04). The world model (and the
hypothesis tree's prior from the CIFAR-100 team) said Muon would not help; it gave the largest gain of the run so far.
Root cause: the prior came from a different code base/tuning; I also underestimated the eager Muon step cost
(launch-bound small kernels, ~2 ms/step) by assuming energy-only costs. Lesson: optimizer quality was the binding
constraint (consistent with E008: the short schedule under-fits); external negative results are weak priors. And
CPU-launch-bound phases DO cost time when they are long enough to leave the GPU idle for whole milliseconds.

H2.8 (open, c=0.45): Resolutions below 24 px are disproportionately cheap (16 px = 0.32x of a 32 px epoch, E026), and the first ~2 epochs tolerate 16 px well. A third 16 px epoch costs more (E029: -0.31 pp for -4.8%, 0.065 pp per 1% of time). With E028's rerun averaged in, the first two 16 px swaps cost ~0.40-0.51 pp for -8.3% (0.05-0.06 pp per 1%), not clearly better than the 24<-32 swap (E025: 0.078). The difference is within ~+/-0.03 pp per 1%. E026 dominates E025 on time only.
H2 (open, c=0.70): Per-epoch GPU work drives the score. The strongest schedule lever is lower-resolution early epochs (E006, E021, E026). Measured epoch cost is stepwise in resolution: 16 px 0.32x, 24 px 0.57x, 26 px 0.75x, 28 px 0.79x, 30 px 0.95x of a 32 px epoch (E013, E026). Narrowing high-FLOP layers is refuted (E017), and so is widening late layers (E005). Under Muon, the optimizer adds a fixed per-step cost (~1 ms/step after compiling, E024) that does not shrink with resolution, so it is a larger share of low-res epochs. Reinvesting low-res savings into more 32 px training is untested under Muon: every Muon schedule so far kept 8 total epochs.

This is an intermediate point between the 24 px bridge, which E032 showed matters (-0.68 pp without it), and a third 16 px epoch (E029, -0.31 pp). The goal is a bridge that costs ~0.40-0.45x instead of 0.57x. Log the compiled per-epoch cost of the 20 px phase.

# E032 · I038 · H2.8

H2.8 (open, c=0.30): Resolutions below 24 px (16-20 px) are disproportionately cheap, because the cost curve is stepwise in post-pool map sizes. Final-map geometry does not matter (E014), so a short first phase at <=20 px costs little accuracy and frees budget for more 24/32 px epochs.
H2 (open, c=0.70): Per-epoch GPU energy (FLOPs + bytes) drives the score on this power-capped GPU: only cutting GPU work shortens training (E004, E010, E011). Lower-resolution epochs are the strongest lever so far (E006: -18.6% time for -0.69 pp, 0.037 pp per 1% of time, about 2x better than cutting full-res epochs). Measured epoch cost is stepwise in resolution (24 px 57%, 26 px 75%, 28 px 79%, 30 px 95% of a 32 px epoch; E013), so the levers are 24 px (or lower) phases and narrower high-FLOP layers, with the saved time reinvested in more total epochs.
Idea: Explore the limit of the very-low-res early phase. On the E026 tip, change only the schedule to 4@16 + 4@32 px (8.0 epochs, no 24 px phase). Same operator, same Muon/SGD hyperparameters, same step count and schedules. 16 px and 32 px shapes are already warmed up in build. This is the aggressive end of the line, beyond the 3@16 + 1@24 variant, and tests whether a 24 px bridge is needed at all.

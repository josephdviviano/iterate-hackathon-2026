## Details (full outcome and post-mortem, condensed above)

### Outcome
accuracy 0.75293 (0.7519, 0.7511, 0.7558) -- infeasible by 0.0001; time 7.219 s vs 7.222 (E001): unchanged.
Per trial prepare/train: 0.100/7.157, 0.019/7.175, 0.018/7.189 vs E001 0.146/7.109, 0.074/7.129, 0.063/7.144.
Prepare fell 70% in steady state (63 -> 18 ms), but train grew by ~the same 45 ms, so per-trial totals are conserved
(7.255/7.20/7.205 in E001, E002 and E004). Verdict refutes H3.2.1: prepare was only ~1% of total, and shrinking it
did not reduce the score. Discarded (also infeasible: the whitening/normalization perturbation moved seeds 0/1 down).
Mechanism found after the run: nvidia-smi sampling during training shows the GPU pinned at its 400 W power cap
(throttle reason 0x4 SW power cap, SM 1335-1380 MHz vs 1410 max). Training is energy-bound: the CPU-bound idle gap in
prepare (dirac loop) let the GPU start train with power headroom; removing the gap just moved the time into train.

### Post-mortem
Predicted time [7.13, 7.20] from "prepare saves 48 ms"; got 7.219 (no change). Root cause: I modeled time as the
sum of independent CPU and GPU parts; in reality the GPU is power-capped, so GPU-idle time is partly "free" (it
banks power headroom) and removing it does not shorten the trial. Lesson: on this power-capped GPU, only reductions in
GPU energy (FLOPs, memory traffic, precision) shorten training; CPU/launch overhead cuts that leave GPU work the same
barely move the score. Corollary for ideas: CUDA graphs / fused optimizers (H3.1) will likely give less than expected;
FLOP cuts (progressive resizing, cheaper layers, fewer epochs) are the lever. Official A100 PCIe is capped at 300 W,
so this applies even more there.
Accuracy: 3-seed mean moves by ~0.2 pp from mere perturbations (whitening subset, fp16 normalization) -> the 0.19 pp
margin of E001 is not a safe buffer; recipe changes need ~+0.2 pp of slack on average.

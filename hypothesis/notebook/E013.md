# E013 · I014 · H2.1.2

H2.1.2 (open, c=0.40): A 9.0-epoch schedule with 26 px for 0-30%, 28 px for 30-60% and 32 px for the last 40% (EfficientTrain++-style long full-res tail), with flip + translate and antialiased downsample, gives 3-seed accuracy >=0.755 and time <=6.7 s (>=7% below A1a's 7.22 s).
H2.1 (open, c=0.60): Progressive resizing makes early epochs much cheaper while costing little final accuracy, if the low-res stage keeps the 3x3 final-pool geometry (>=26 px), uses a scale-preserving operator (crop or antialiased downsample of the flip+translate image, not random-resized crop), and keeps a meaningful full-res tail. Each shape is compiled and warmed up in build.
H2 (open, c=0.65): Per-epoch GPU compute drives the score. About 95% of GPU time is in conv, BN and activation kernels, and on this power-capped GPU time scales roughly with FLOPs/bytes. So cutting per-epoch FLOPs (lower early resolution, narrower high-resolution layers) reduces time, provided accuracy is recovered.
Idea: Start from A1a (E001 tip) and add progressive resizing with a long full-res tail. Total 9.0 epochs (LR/EMA/lookahead schedules rescaled to the step count as the recipe already does). Train at 26 px for steps 0-30%, 28 px for 30-60% and 32 px for the last 40%. Low-res batches are made by applying the existing flip + translate-2 at 32 px, then F.interpolate(mode='bilinear', antialias=True) to 26/28 px. No random-resized crop. The final group pool must still end at 3x3 (26->25->12->6->3, 28->27->13->6->3). In build, compile and warm up all 3 train shapes plus eval at 32 px, so nothing recompiles in timed code. Instrumentation only: log a hash of the first batch's augmentation parameters per trial.
Expected (from the queue): Time about 6.3-6.5 s, -10 to -13% vs A1a's 7.22 s. Per-epoch costs from E006: 26 px ~0.56, 28 px ~0.65, 32 px ~0.84 s. If 0.749-0.753, follow with 9.5 epochs and the same fractions (about +0.35 s).

## Details (full outcome and post-mortem, condensed above)

### Outcome
accuracy 0.75057 (0.7525, 0.7489, 0.7503) vs tip 0.75493 (-0.44 pp); time 6.665 s vs 7.222 (-7.7%). Per-step
resolution: 26 px steps 0-129, 28 px 130-259, 32 px 260-431. Verdict refutes H2.1.2 (accuracy < 0.751 bar, and far
from 0.755). Exchange rate 0.057 pp per 1% time: better than cutting epochs (0.08, E007) but worse than E006 (0.037).
Contingency "right way but less than predicted" for time (6.665 vs ~6.4 predicted).
Measured per-resolution epoch cost after the run (2 epochs per size, compiled, same recipe; s/epoch):
  24 px 0.485 (57%) | 26 px 0.632 (75%) | 28 px 0.671 (79%) | 30 px 0.808 (95%) | 32 px 0.848 (100%).
The curve is stepwise, not quadratic: cost follows the post-pool map sizes (32: 31/15/7/3, 30: 29/14/7/3,
28: 27/13/6/3, 26: 25/12/6/3, 24: 23/11/5/2). 24 px is the sweet spot (43% cheaper); 26-28 px save only 21-25%,
30 px ~5%. Turning off dynamo's automatic dynamic shapes (static graph per size) changed nothing (within 3%).
Implication: E006's gain came mostly from its 4 epochs at 24 px. Schedules should use 24 px (not 26/28) for the cheap
phase and spend the rest at 32 px, e.g. 9 ep = 4 @24 + 5 @32 (~6.3 s est.) or 5 @24 + 4 @32 (~5.9 s est.).

### Post-mortem
Time predicted [6.25, 6.60], got 6.665. Root cause: I (and the idea) assumed epoch cost scales with pixel count
(26 px ~ 0.66x); measured cost is 0.75x at 26 px and 0.79x at 28 px because conv cost after pooling follows integer
map sizes (12x12/6x6 vs 15x15/7x7) and small maps use the GPU less efficiently. Accuracy predicted [0.749, 0.756] (hit
at the low end). Lesson: measure the cost curve before designing schedules; 24 px is the efficient low-res point.

# E045 · I040 · H2.4.1

H2.4.1 (open, c=0.60): NVML sampling at >=10 Hz during an untimed run of the E026 tip shows board power >=95% of the 400 W cap for >=80% of the 32 px phase. During the 16 px and 24 px phases power stays below 90% of the cap for >=30% of the time, i.e. those phases are launch- or per-step-bound. Observational only; no sleeps or idle gaps added.
H2.4 (open, c=0.50): At full resolution the A100-SXM4 trains at its 400 W cap, so time for the net's fwd/bwd tracks GPU energy, and CPU gaps hidden behind GPU work cost nothing (E004, E010, E011; all SGD, 32 px). This does NOT hold in the small-map 16/24 px phases. There, bs 1024->1536 saved ~2 ms per removed step (E038), twice the ~1 ms Muon cost. Per-step work beyond Muon (SGD foreach step, lookahead, launch gaps, small-kernel inefficiency) therefore costs wall time there. Transfer risk: on the official 300 W PCIe card, GPU work is slower, so per-step and launch overhead is a smaller share. Levers whose gain is per-step (batch size, CUDA graphs) are less likely to transfer than FLOP-reducing levers. Prefer FLOP-reducing levers on local ties.
H2 (open, c=0.70): Per-epoch GPU work and per-step overhead drive the score. The strongest schedule lever is cheap early epochs: 2@16 + 2@24 + 4@32 px (E026), and 24 px is the minimum useful bridge (E032, E041). Measured epoch cost relative to 32 px: 16 px 0.32x, 20 px ~0.51x, 24 px 0.57x, 26 px 0.75x, 28 px 0.79x, 30 px 0.95x (E013, E026, E041). Narrowing (E017) and widening (E005) were refuted only under SGD. In the low-res phases a per-step cost (~2 ms/step at bs 1024, E038) is a large share, which is why bs 1536 paid off. Every Muon schedule so far kept 8 total epochs and a single global batch size.
Idea: Observational, on the E026 tip with no recipe change (replacing the stale I033, which assumed eager Muon on E021). During one scored run, sample board power and SM clock via NVML at >=10 Hz in a separate monitoring process launched outside the submission (no sleeps in submission code). Tag samples by phase (16 px, 24 px, 32 px) using logged CUDA-event timestamps. Report, per phase, the fraction of time at >=95% of the 400 W cap and the mean SM clock.
Expected (from the queue): Accuracy and time identical to E026 (±0.5%). The 32 px phase sits at the cap for >=80% of its time. The 16 px phase sits below 90% of the cap for >=30% of its time (small maps, per-step Muon and launch costs dominate). If so, the early phase is overhead-bound, and per-step fixes (bs 2000 in the 16 px phase only, fewer NS steps) pay off there specifically.

# E021 · I023 · H2.1

H2.1 (open, c=0.60): Progressive resizing makes early epochs much cheaper at little final-accuracy cost, provided the cheap phase is at the efficient 24 px point (57% of a 32 px epoch; 26-28 px save only 21-25%) and the savings are reinvested in clearly more total epochs (Fable's CIFAR-10 recipe: ~+16% epochs). The low-res operator (antialiased downsample vs crop, E016) and final-map geometry (2x2 vs 3x3, E014) are second-order. The E006 operator is flip + translate-2 at 32 px followed by an antialiased bilinear downsample; no random-resized crop was ever used. Every shape is compiled and warmed up in build.
H2 (open, c=0.70): Per-epoch GPU energy (FLOPs + bytes) drives the score on this power-capped GPU: only cutting GPU work shortens training (E004, E010, E011). Lower-resolution epochs are the strongest lever so far (E006: -18.6% time for -0.69 pp, 0.037 pp per 1% of time, about 2x better than cutting full-res epochs). Measured epoch cost is stepwise in resolution (24 px 57%, 26 px 75%, 28 px 79%, 30 px 95% of a 32 px epoch; E013), so the levers are 24 px (or lower) phases and narrower high-FLOP layers, with the saved time reinvested in more total epochs.
Idea: Port the two-stage 24->32 px progressive schedule to the E019 Muon recipe. 8.0 total epochs: epochs 0-3 at 24 px, epochs 4-7 at 32 px, no 28 px phase. Use the E006 operator: flip + translate-2 at 32 px, then F.interpolate(bilinear, antialias=True) to 24 px, with AdaptiveMaxPool2d(1) as the final pool. Rescale the LR/EMA/lookahead schedules to the step count. Keep the Muon step eager as in E019. In build, compile and warm up both train shapes and the 32 px eval, so nothing recompiles in timed code.

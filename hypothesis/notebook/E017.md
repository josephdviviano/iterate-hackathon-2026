# E017 · I019 · H2.6.1

H2.6.1 (open, c=0.20): At 8.5 epochs, group 2 width 384->320 (or group 1 width 128->96) cuts time by >=6% with a 3-seed accuracy loss <=0.3 pp versus A1a.
H2.6 (open, c=0.25): FLOPs are concentrated in the higher-resolution groups (group 2 at 15x15 with 384 channels and group 1 at 31x31 carry most conv FLOPs). Narrowing them cuts per-epoch energy at unchanged eval resolution, which makes it the architectural analogue of progressive resizing.
H2 (open, c=0.65): Per-epoch GPU compute drives the score. About 95% of GPU time is in conv, BN and activation kernels, and on this power-capped GPU time scales roughly with FLOPs/bytes. So cutting per-epoch FLOPs (lower early resolution, narrower high-resolution layers) reduces time, provided accuracy is recovered.
Idea: On A1a (8.5 epochs, everything else unchanged), narrow group 2 from 384 to 320 channels, which also shrinks group 3's first conv input. Recompile and warm up in build. Group 2 at 15x15 carries ~50% of conv FLOPs by a rough count. 384->320 cuts total conv FLOPs by about 15%. The exchange rate to beat is the epoch-cut rate, 0.08 pp per 1% of time (E007).
Expected (from the queue): Time about 6.4-6.7 s (-8 to -12%). Accuracy 0.746-0.752 (-0.3 to -0.8 pp). If the loss per 1% of time saved is <0.06 pp, it beats epoch cuts and is worth combining with +0.5 epochs or with progressive resizing. Otherwise H2.6 is refuted (capacity is not cheap to remove on CIFAR-100).

### Post-mortem
Predicted accuracy [0.745, 0.753] (hit at the bottom), time [6.45, 6.80] (hit). The hypothesis that width at 15x15 is
"cheap to remove" failed: removing 15% of FLOPs from group 2 cost nearly 1 pp. Lesson: on CIFAR-100 the network is
capacity-limited at this size (consistent with E005: adding capacity to group 3 also wasn't cheap enough, but
removing it is expensive). The best lever remains training at reduced resolution early (E006), which keeps capacity.

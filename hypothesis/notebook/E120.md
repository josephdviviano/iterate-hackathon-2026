H2.20 (supported, c=0.60): Late layer freezing is a real time lever with a small, real accuracy cost. Detaching group 1's output (no backward into group 1 or the stem) from 85% of samples saved 3.6% scored (4.5% dev) at -0.12 pp vs E113 arm A, or ~-0.09 pp vs the pooled 200-trajectory E100 baseline (~3 SE). That is ~0.025-0.033 pp per 1%, as good as or better than any measured trade. External priors are consistent: MosaicML ~5% throughput with no Pareto gain, FreezeOut null on VGG-like nets. So it is a rate lever, not free. Open: the shape of the dose-response, whether a stochastic/progressive freeze trades better, and whether the loss fits within the accepted population margin.
H2 (open, c=0.50): Per-epoch GPU work drives the score. Falsifiable core: in the energy-bound 32 px phase (~71% of time), wall time tracks activation bytes moved in backward more than FLOPs. So cutting backward through the high-resolution early layers (stem + group 1 at 31x31) saves more than their FLOP share, while width cuts in the low-resolution group 2 save less than theirs (E080: 33-60% recovered). E116 is the first supporting point: freezing stem + group 1 over the last 15% of samples saved 3.6-4.5% of total time, implying a ~15-20% cheaper 32 px step vs a ~13% backward FLOP share. Tip E100 is ~4.46 s (pop. mean ~0.7530). Closed time levers: CUDA graphs, Inductor tuning, cuDNN determinism, benchmark_limit, prepare, group-2 width, whitening-bias freeze, 32 px trims at a poorly measured rate, and the 28 px substitution. Live: the late-freeze dose-response (H2.20), group-1 width (N_g1w), depth cuts (H2.18, low), group-3 width (H2.19, low).

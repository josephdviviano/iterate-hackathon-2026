H12.1 (open, c=0.08): Multi-sample augmentation at equal compute: 2+ augmented copies of the same image within a minibatch at fixed batch size and step count. Fort et al. (2105.13343) find +1.6 pp on CIFAR-100 at multiplicity 4, but only at ~8,000 sample-epochs, where coverage is not a constraint. At ~7.5 epochs, halving unique-image coverage is a real risk, and alternating flip already lowers augmentation variance. Low prior.
H12 (open, c=0.15): Under Muon, per-batch data composition and augmentation, not scalar optimizer knobs, may be the remaining zero-time accuracy sources. Every augmentation test (E008, E016) ran under SGD. External priors are weak: multi-sample augmentation gains on CIFAR-100 appear only at ~8,000 sample-epochs (Fort et al.), stronger translate/Cutout appears only in airbench96's 37-40-epoch recipe, and no speed recipe uses class-balanced batches. Order: H12.2 (no downside), then H12.3, then H12.1. All candidates are judged on the large-seed paired instrument (H4.6.1), because their predicted effects (~0.1 pp) are below the 22-seed resolution.
Idea: Multi-sample augmentation in the 32 px phase, at equal compute. Base: E100 code. In the 2.75-epoch 32 px phase only (bs 1024), build each batch from 512 unique images, drawn as the next 512 of the epoch permutation, times 2 augmentation draws. Batch shape, step count, LR/EMA/tail indexing and the compiled graphs are unchanged; only the gather indices and the augmentation parameters differ. The 32 px phase then covers ~1.375 unique epochs instead of 2.75. The 16/24/28 px phases are unchanged. Smoke-test that the duplicated indices and the flip parity are correct on one batch. Decide on off-seed seeds 3-26 in >=3 fresh processes against E106, plus one scored sample, and a second only if off-seed >= +0.05 pp.

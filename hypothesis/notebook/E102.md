H11.1 (open, c=0.30): Resolution-statistics adaptation. The antialiased downsample changes patch statistics, so each resolution switch shifts the stem-output distribution. The 64x-LR whitening bias and the BN affine parameters absorb that shift. Freezing the bias before the later switches removes this adaptation. Evidence: E093 (freezing at 0.2 epoch cost -0.44 pp off-seed and -0.5 pp scored, the largest clearly real recent effect). The current freeze sits at ~41% of samples, inside the 24 px phase, before the 28 and 32 px switches.
H11 (open, c=0.40): Accuracy at the tip is bounded by late training at test resolution, i.e. 32 px samples × updates in the final phase, not by total steps or total epochs. Every way of shrinking the 32 px phase costs the same ~0.05-0.065 pp per 1% of time: the trim (E089), the equal-step 28 px substitution (E090), and the whole-epoch shift (E025). Adding updates inside the 32 px phase helps (E084/E085/E095). This competes with H10's placement-independence and with H2's view that time is the only thing that differs between phases. Two candidate mechanisms: (a) the net must re-adapt to the test-resolution input statistics after each resolution switch (stem output, BN statistics, the whitening bias); (b) full-resolution samples carry fine-detail information that the downsampled phases cannot supply.

H2.9.1 (open, c=0.25): Replacing exact GELU with GELU(approximate='tanh') on A1a reduces time by >=1% with a 3-seed accuracy within 0.25 pp of A1a (>=0.7525).
H2.9 (open, c=0.30): GELU's exact erf costs measurable energy in the fused BN+activation kernels (E003: SiLU saved 2.2%). A cheaper GELU approximation (tanh or polynomial) keeps GELU's accuracy advantage while saving part of that cost, and stacks on any schedule.
H2 (open, c=0.70): Per-epoch GPU energy (FLOPs + bytes) drives the score on this power-capped GPU: only cutting GPU work shortens training (E004, E010, E011). Lower-resolution epochs are the strongest lever so far (E006: -18.6% time for -0.69 pp, 0.037 pp per 1% of time, about 2x better than cutting full-res epochs). Measured epoch cost is stepwise in resolution (24 px 57%, 26 px 75%, 28 px 79%, 30 px 95% of a 32 px epoch; E013), so the levers are 24 px (or lower) phases and narrower high-FLOP layers, with the saved time reinvested in more total epochs.

## Details (full outcome and post-mortem, condensed above)

### Outcome
accuracy 0.7663 (0.7667, 0.7673, 0.7649) = E019 (0.76633); time 8.452 s vs 8.041: +5.1% SLOWER. Verdict refutes
H2.9.1. The tanh approximation costs more than exact erf in Inductor's fused BN+activation kernels (x^3, tanh and
extra multiplies in fwd and especially bwd), and under the power cap that extra arithmetic is paid in full.
Contingency "opposite way": keep exact GELU. Elementwise micro-tuning is done (E003 SiLU: faster but -0.6 pp).

### Post-mortem
Predicted time [7.88, 8.05] -> miss (8.45). I assumed "tanh approx is cheaper than erf" from CPU/older-GPU folklore;
on A100 with Inductor, erf is a single fast intrinsic while the tanh form adds several ops, and the backward of the
tanh form is longer still. Lesson: activation cost must be measured, not assumed; ~5% of the run is in GELU fwd/bwd
arithmetic, so the activation is a real energy consumer -- but only an activation that is both cheaper and as
accurate as GELU would help, and none tested so far is.

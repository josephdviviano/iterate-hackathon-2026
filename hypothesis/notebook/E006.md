# E006 · I007 · H2.1.1

H2.1.1 (open, c=0.40): A 24->28->32 px schedule, with at most +0.5 epochs to compensate, cuts time by >=5% compared with the best fixed-32 px recipe while keeping accuracy >=0.753.
H2.1 (open, c=0.50): Progressive resizing (24 -> 28 -> 32 px) makes early epochs much cheaper while costing little final accuracy. Each shape must be compiled and warmed up in build to avoid timed recompiles.
H2 (open, c=0.60): Per-epoch compute drives the score. About 95% of GPU time is in conv, BN and activation kernels, so cutting FLOPs or kernel cost per epoch (lower early resolution, a cheaper activation, training on fewer examples) reduces time at constant accuracy.
Idea: On top of A1a, train at 24 px for the first ~40% of epochs, 28 px for the next ~30% and 32 px for the rest, with random-resized crops on the GPU. Add +0.5 epochs (9.0 total). Compile and warm up all three shapes in build so no recompile happens in timed code. The final EMA/eval runs at 32 px.
Expected (from the queue): Time -5 to -10% vs A1a. Accuracy -0.1 to -0.4 pp (risk of dropping below 0.753, explore).

## Details (full outcome and post-mortem, condensed above)

### Outcome
accuracy 0.7480 (0.7480, 0.7479, 0.7481) vs 0.75493 (-0.69 pp); time 5.880 s vs 7.222 (-18.6%). Schedule at 9.0
epochs: epochs 0-3 @24 px, 4-6 @28 px, 7-8 @32 px. Infeasible -> discard; verdict inconclusive (time gain far above
the 5% bar, accuracy between the refute bar 0.745 and the 0.753 target). Contingency "in range, acc 0.745-0.753" fired:
progressive resizing has large headroom -- this is the best accuracy/time exchange rate seen so far:
-0.69 pp for -1.34 s, vs ~+0.3-0.5 pp per extra full-res epoch costing ~0.85 s.
Rough per-epoch costs implied: 24 px ~0.47 s, 28 px ~0.65 s, 32 px ~0.84 s.
Most promising follow-ups (to recover ~0.7 pp while keeping most of the -18%): (a) 10 epochs with the same fractions
(~+0.6 s, est. ~6.5 s), (b) keep 9 epochs but shorten the 24 px phase (e.g. 24 px until 25%, 28 px until 55%),
(c) end with 3 epochs at 32 px. Also check whether 24->32 directly (skip 28) or the antialias interpolation matters.
Implementation note (exact operator, for later ideas): NO random-resized crop was used. Each epoch's images were made
with the recipe's usual flip + translate-2 at 32 px, then (for 24/28 px epochs) the whole epoch was downsampled with
F.interpolate(mode="bilinear", antialias=True). The final nn.MaxPool2d(3) was replaced by nn.AdaptiveMaxPool2d(1)
(identical at 32 px; at 24 px the last map is 2x2, at 28 px 3x3). Resolution switched per epoch (epoch start fraction).
Compile handled 3 shapes x 2 whiten-bias variants without timed recompiles (trial times 5.92/5.86/5.86).

### Post-mortem
Predicted accuracy [0.735, 0.753] (hit, 0.748) and time [5.6, 6.4] (hit, 5.88). Failure = infeasible. Root cause:
the schedule from the idea spends 7 of 9 epochs below 32 px (4 @24, 3 @28); the network's 32 px features only get 2
epochs. The world model had no estimate of the accuracy cost per low-res epoch. Measured: -0.69 pp for -1.34 s,
an exchange rate ~2x better than cutting full-res epochs (E007). Lesson: progressive resizing is the strongest lever
so far; the schedule (fraction at low res, total epochs) needs tuning to land just above 0.753, not a different idea.

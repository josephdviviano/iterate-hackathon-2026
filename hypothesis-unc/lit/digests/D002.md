**Digest: E011 (20→24→28→32 px) came in faster and less accurate than all four forecasts predicted**

**The result.** E011 ran in 5.70 s, below every forecaster's low bound. Accuracy was 0.7451, also below every low bound. So the schedule gave more speed than expected and cost more accuracy than expected.

**Accuracy: the network gets too little time at full resolution.** The FixRes papers explain the accuracy shortfall best.
- **C0009 and C0013:** if training sees objects at a different apparent size than testing does, accuracy drops. Here, downscaling the whole image to 20 or 24 px shrinks objects in pixel terms. The test set is always 32 px, and the global max-pool head has to bridge that gap.
- **C0011:** a short phase of fine-tuning at the test resolution recovers the lost accuracy. That phase is what E011 cut: 30% of steps at 32 px in E007, only 20% in E011. With the LR and EMA averaging both running over a shorter full-resolution stretch, the weights and BatchNorm statistics probably adapt less to 32 px.
- **Status:** C0011 *explains* the result. This mechanism, not "fewer pixels overall", is what the tree's H2.3 should be about.
- **Next step:** keep the 20/24 px start but restore the 32 px phase to 30% or more. Separately, test recalibrating BatchNorm stats at 32 px (H1.3.6).
- **Caveat:** the FixRes evidence comes from ImageNet with pretrained or fine-tuned models, so the size of the effect here is *conditional*.

**A mechanism the tree lacks.**
- **C0019 and C0020 (EfficientNetV2):** progressive resizing on its own lowers accuracy. The fix is to scale regularization with image size: weaker augmentation and dropout at small sizes, stronger at large sizes.
- Our recipe applies the same regularization at every size: label smoothing 0.3, crop augmentation and alternating flip. At 20 px that likely over-regularizes, which would explain why the accuracy loss grew faster than the forecasters expected.
- **Status:** *untested* here.
- **Next step:** add an H2.3 child that lowers label smoothing (and/or crop strength) in the low-resolution phases and keeps it at 0.3 at 32 px. Compare against E011 at the same time cost.

**Time: why it beat the pixel-count model.** None of the cards explains the extra speed.
- If time scaled with pixel count, E011 would take about 0.847 of E007's time (654 vs 773 pixel-units per sample), or about 6.2 s.
- It measured 5.70 s, which is more than proportional. If there were a fixed per-step overhead, the speedup would be *less* than proportional, so this hints that very small feature maps are especially cheap per pixel (for example, better cache use or a different kernel choice).
- **C0016** only says in general terms that training at reduced resolution is efficient.
- **Next step:** profile time per step at each resolution before forecasting the next schedule. Don't assume a linear pixel model.

**Not relevant.**
- **C0007 and C0008:** fine-tuning on high-resolution x-rays (128–900 px). Their wall-time conclusion runs the opposite way to ours and has no bearing here.
- **C0012, C0014, C0015, C0017 and C0018:** these address test-resolution increases, overfitting, normalization type and group convolutions. None of them touches E011.

**What this means for the tree.** The literature backs E011's discard. It suggests the accuracy budget should go to a longer final phase at 32 px rather than to an even more aggressive start. It also suggests adding size-dependent regularization as a new mechanism under H2.3.

## Claims
- C0011 (explains; H2.3, H2.3.1): E011 cut the 32 px phase from 30% to 20% of steps, leaving too little time to adapt to the test resolution. Next: keep the low-resolution start but restore the 32 px tail to 30% or more.
- C0009 (explains; H2.3): A mismatch between the object sizes seen in training and at test lowers accuracy. Downscaling whole images to 20 px widens that mismatch for the max-pool head. Recast H2.3 around this mismatch rather than total FLOPs.
- C0013 (conditional; H2.3): Shows how sampling differences cause the train/test mismatch. The evidence is from ImageNet RandomResizedCrop, so the effect size here is uncertain.
- C0010 (conditional; H2.3): FixRes found lower-resolution training can help, but only where train crops make objects look larger. Here downscaling makes objects look smaller, so the benefit should not be expected.
- C0019 (explains; H2.3): Progressive resizing on its own costs accuracy, consistent with E007's small loss and E011's larger one.
- C0020 (untested; H2.3, H1.3): New mechanism: scale regularization with image size. Add an H2.3 child with lower label smoothing or weaker crops at 20-24 px and LS 0.3 at 32 px, compared with E011 at the same time.
- C0016 (conditional; H2.3): Generic support that reduced-resolution training improves efficiency. It does not explain why E011's time fell more than pixel count predicts; profile per-step time at each resolution.

**Digest: E025 (Lookahead alpha exponent 3 → 2)**

**The surprise is in time, and the cause is probably the base, not the method.** All four forecasters put time at about 6.55 s, which is E017's time at about 9.5 true epochs. E025 measured 6.2017 s. That is almost exactly E023's 6.198 s, which is 9.0 true epochs. The I038 text says "E017 recipe (or I033's if kept)", but E023 was the tip when the run happened. Changing the alpha exponent touches only a scalar every 5 steps, so it should cost no time. The most likely explanation is that E025 was built on E023, or forecast against the wrong base. **Check the step count in commit 1223459 before reading anything else into this.** None of the cards below say anything about wall time. The literature has nothing on the time miss.

**Accuracy.** If E023 is the base, E025's seeds 0–2 mean is 0.7557 vs E023's 0.7538, a gain of 0.19 pp. That is below the roughly 0.4 pp that a single 3-seed comparison can resolve (H4.1). The run was still discarded, which suggests the held-out seeds 3–12 mean was worse. Record that number to answer the open crux. It does not support the claim that "averaging is under-tuned".

**What the literature says:**
- **C0021** (EMA lets SGD converge at a higher final LR, without decaying to near zero; tested with ResNet-18 on CIFAR-100). This **explains** E009: decaying LR to 0 added nothing on top of EMA. It also suggests that when averaging is already active, its schedule is a flat dial, which fits E025's null result. Action: close H1.3.4 and stop spending runs on the end of the LR schedule.
- **C0022 / C0023** (EMA reaches flatter, different minima that generalize better). This **explains** E005's gain of about 1 pp in general terms. It says nothing about the ramp exponent. No action.
- **C0024 / C0025** (Switch EMA: copying the EMA weights back into the model each epoch beats plain EMA and speeds convergence). Airbench-style Lookahead already does this, copying the averaged weights back every 5 steps. That makes the card **conditional** evidence against H1.3.5: the trajectory reset may carry part of the gain, not just endpoint averaging. The tested regime was long multi-task training, not runs of about 9 epochs. Action: if H1.3.5 is run, expect plain EMA to land below Lookahead. A cheap new child worth adding is the reset interval (every 5 steps vs every 10–25) at a fixed exponent.

**What this means for the tree.** H1.3's "longer or stronger averaging" branch looks used up. Gains under about 0.2 pp cannot be resolved without held-out seeds. Put effort into the step-count and backward-truncation cruxes instead (I043, H2.6.1).

## Claims
- C0021 (explains; H1.3.4, H1.3): EMA lets training converge at a higher final LR. This explains why E009's decay to 0 added nothing on top of EMA, and why the averaging schedule (E025) is a flat dial. Close H1.3.4.
- C0022 (explains; H1.3): General mechanism for E005's gain of about 1 pp (EMA reaches different, better-generalizing minima). Says nothing about the ramp exponent.
- C0023 (explains; H1.3): Flat-optima view of weight averaging. Supports H1.3's parent claim only. No action.
- C0024 (conditional; H1.3.5): Switching the EMA weights back into the model (which Lookahead already does every 5 steps) beats plain EMA. Expect H1.3.5's plain EMA to fall short of Lookahead. The tested regime was long training, so this is untested at about 9 epochs. Consider a reset-interval child.
- C0025 (untested; H1.3): SEMA's claimed faster convergence suggests the reset interval, not the alpha exponent, as the untuned averaging knob. Proposes a new H1.3 child (interval 5 vs 10–25 steps).

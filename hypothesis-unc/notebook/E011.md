# E011 · I011 · H2.3

H2.3 (open, c=0.45): Training early epochs at lower resolution (progressive resizing) cuts FLOPs per epoch more than it cuts accuracy.
H2 (open, c=0.75): At a fixed epoch count, the cost per epoch (systems overhead plus FLOPs per sample) is a large lever. Python and launch overhead under the 4-CPU pin, the data pipeline, precision, and input resolution can be cut by 10-25% without hurting accuracy.
Idea: Exploit, building on E007's finding that time scales with pixel count. On the E007 recipe at 9 epochs, use a more aggressive resolution schedule: the first 30% of steps at 20 px, then 30% at 24 px, 20% at 28 px, and the last 20% at 32 px. Each resolution is compiled and warmed up in build with channels_last synthetic batches of the exact training shape. Use error_on_recompile after build. Keep the global max-pool head so the network works at every size.
Expected (from the queue): Time falls 6-10% (to about 6.6-6.9 s). Accuracy falls 0.2-0.5 pp (to about 0.754-0.757). The idea succeeds if the time saved per pp lost is better than the epoch trim's, about 5% per 0.3 pp. A loss above 0.6 pp means 20 px is too coarse for 100 classes.

## Post-mortem
<!-- For failures and missed predictions: what you predicted, what happened, the root cause,
what the world model got wrong, the lesson. -->
Predicted accuracy [0.746, 0.7545] and time [5.8, 6.3]; got 0.7451 and 5.70 (both slightly low). Time: I assumed a sizeable per-step fixed cost (Muon NS, optimizer, EMA, Python) that wouldn't shrink with resolution; in fact train time fell 14.6% for a 15.4% fall in pixel-weighted cost. So ~95% of train time is pixel-proportional conv work and fixed per-step overhead is only ~5%. Accuracy: the loss is ~4x the E007 resizing loss per unit of pixel saved. Two changes were confounded: the 20 px phase and the shorter full-res tail (30% -> 20%). The world model treated resolution as a smooth dial; it has a cliff. Coarse early resolution or a short 32 px tail costs a lot on 100 classes. Lesson: (1) per-step overhead work (CUDA graphs, optimizer fusion) can save at most ~5%; (2) keep >= 30% of steps at 32 px. Any further resizing should test one factor at a time (e.g. keep the tail at 30% and only add 20 px early).

H2.3.6 (open, c=0.35): An early low resolution is close to free. On the E026 tip, extending the 20 px phase from 15% to 30% of steps (taken from the 24 px phase; the 28/32 px phases keep their step counts and positions) cuts mean time by at least 2.5% and loses no more than 0.15 pp of the held-out seeds 3–12 mean.
H2.3 (supported, c=0.70): Progressive resizing is a near-linear time dial for tile-friendly sizes (about 0.51/0.71/0.89 s per epoch at 24/28/32 px, E012). Accuracy is carried by the 28 and 32 px phases. E007's 24/28/32 at 40/30/30 is cheap, and so is a short early 20 px phase (E026: −0.15 pp held-out, ~1.5σ, unresolved). Refuted variants: E018 (drop the 28 px phase: −0.68 pp held-out, ~0.8 s/pp), E011 (20 px start plus a 20% 32 px tail: −0.97 pp on 3 seeds, confounded) and E024 (26 px middle phase: −0.25 pp for −2%, ~0.5 s/pp, off-grid tiles; see H6.2). These record the Lvl3 refutes that were logged against this Lvl2. Candidate mechanisms: (a) low resolution during the peak-LR window; (b) a FixRes object-scale and BN-statistics shift at the final jump; (c) too little full-resolution fine-tuning while LR decays and averaging forms (C0011). The attribution of E011's loss to the 32 px tail comes from subtracting a 3-seed number from a held-out number and is unproven (H2.3.7 is the control).

Launch overhead is exhausted (E015, E017).

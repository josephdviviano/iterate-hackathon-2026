H2.3.2 (open, c=0.22): On the E016 recipe, adding a 20 px phase over the first ~15% of steps while keeping at least 30% of steps at 32 px (single-factor change vs E011) cuts mean time by at least 4% and loses no more than 0.25 pp of 3-seed mean accuracy.
H2.3 (supported, c=0.60): Progressive resizing is a near-linear time dial: step time scales with pixel count (about 0.51/0.71/0.89 s per epoch at 24/28/32 px, E012). Its accuracy cost is mild only for the E007 schedule (24/28/32 at 40/30/30). Every more aggressive variant has an exchange rate of about 0.8–1.0 s/pp, worse than the epoch dial at about 1.5 s/pp: E011 added 20 px and cut the 32 px tail to 20%; E018 dropped 28 px (−0.68 pp held-out). Candidate mechanisms: (a) low resolution during the peak-LR window, where most features are learned; (b) a large final resolution jump that shifts object scale and BN statistics (FixRes); (c) E007's own cost was underestimated because E006 was a lucky 3-seed draw.
H2 (supported, c=0.85): At a fixed epoch count, the cost per epoch (systems overhead plus FLOPs per sample) is a large lever. Python and launch overhead under the 4-CPU pin, the data pipeline, precision, and input resolution can be cut by 10-25% without hurting accuracy.
Idea: Explore. This is the single-factor version of E011, separating its two confounded changes. On the E016 recipe, change only the first phase: the first 15% of steps run at 20 px, followed by 24 px for 25%, 28 px for 30% and 32 px for 30%. The 32 px tail keeps its exact step count and position, and the LR and EMA schedules are unchanged. Warm up and capture the 20 px graph in build with channels_last tensors from the training code path, with error_on_recompile on.

Accuracy changes by -0.05 to -0.35 pp. A loss of 0.2 pp or less with time down at least 4% supports H2.3.2 and pins E011's cliff on the shortened 32 px tail, not on the 20 px phase.

# E007 · I008 · H2.3.1

H2.3.1 (open, c=0.45): A 24→28→32 px schedule, with every resolution warmed up in build, saves at least 5% of mean time and loses no more than 0.2 pp of accuracy at the same epoch count.
H2.3 (open, c=0.50): Training early epochs at lower resolution (progressive resizing) cuts FLOPs per epoch more than it cuts accuracy.
H2 (open, c=0.65): At a fixed epoch count, the cost per epoch (systems overhead plus FLOPs per sample) is a large lever. Python and launch overhead under the 4-CPU pin, the data pipeline, precision, and input resolution can be cut by 10-25% without hurting accuracy.
Idea: Explore. On the port, apply progressive resizing: the first about 40% of epochs at 24 px, the next 30% at 28 px, the rest at 32 px (random-resized-crop on the GPU via F.interpolate). Warm up every resolution in build on synthetic data. Epoch count unchanged.
Expected (from the queue): Time drops 5-15%. Accuracy changes between -0.5 and +0.1 pp; a loss above 0.2 pp would refute H2.3.1.

Time predicted [7.4, 8.6], got 7.33 (slightly faster than the low end). Root cause: I added ~0.1 s fixed cost for interpolate and prepare and assumed low-res kernels would be less efficient.

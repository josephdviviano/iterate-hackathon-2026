# E025 · I038 · H1.3

H1.3 (supported, c=0.80): In short schedules on CIFAR-100, regularization that sharpens the end-of-training solution raises accuracy at a fixed epoch budget, and the gain can be traded for fewer epochs. Candidates are stronger label smoothing (about 0.3) and Lookahead/EMA of weights late in training.
H1 (supported, c=0.85): The number of epochs needed to reach 75% (sample efficiency) sets most of the wall time. Backbone, optimizer and regularization choices that cut epochs-to-target are the main lever, about 7x over the 40-epoch ResNet9 baseline (59.3 s).
Idea: Exploit, as a time-neutral accuracy booster to fund trims (W1 crux: does a stronger or longer EMA add 0.3 pp?). E005 showed end-of-run averaging is worth about 1 pp on CIFAR-100, and E009 showed a lower final LR adds nothing on top of it, so averaging strength is the untuned part. On the E017 recipe (or I033's if kept), change only the Lookahead alpha ramp exponent from 3 to 2: alpha = 0.95**5 * (step/total)**2 instead of **3. The averaging then pulls harder from about mid-run on. Update frequency (every 5 steps), the final full copy, LR schedule, epochs and resolution all stay unchanged. Alpha is a per-step tensor or constant, so recapture the graphs if it is baked in. Judge on held-out seeds 3–12 as well as seeds 0–2. This proposes a new H1.3 child: the airbench96 averaging schedule is under-tuned for CIFAR-100 short runs.
Expected (from the queue): Time unchanged (±1%). Held-out mean moves between −0.2 and +0.35 pp from E017's 0.7577. A held-out gain of at least 0.2 pp supports the child and funds about 0.5 epoch more of trimming. A loss of 0.15 pp or more means mid-run pull-back slows feature learning, so the next point to test is exponent 4.

# E018 · I023 · H2.3

H2.3 (supported, c=0.75): Progressive resizing is a near-linear time dial: step time scales with pixel count (24/28/32 px ≈ 0.51/0.71/0.89 s per epoch, E012). It costs little accuracy while early phases stay at ≥24 px and ≥30% of steps run at 32 px (E007: −22% time, −0.59 pp). Accuracy falls off a cliff when a 20 px phase is added and the 32 px tail is cut to 20% (E011: −0.97 pp). Candidate mechanisms for the cliff are train/test object-size mismatch with too little full-resolution fine-tuning (FixRes), over-regularization at small sizes, and EMA/BN stats contaminated by low-res phases.
H2 (supported, c=0.85): At a fixed epoch count, the cost per epoch (systems overhead plus FLOPs per sample) is a large lever. Python and launch overhead under the 4-CPU pin, the data pipeline, precision, and input resolution can be cut by 10-25% without hurting accuracy.
Idea: Exploit. Tests a proposed new child of H2.3: accuracy is set mainly in the final 32 px phase, so the 28 px middle phase can be dropped cheaply. On the E015 recipe (Muon, Lookahead/EMA, CUDA graphs, 9 epochs, last group 512), change only the resolution schedule from 24/28/32 px at 40/30/30% of steps to 24/32 px at 70/30% of steps. The 32 px tail keeps exactly its current step count and position, so the LR and EMA schedules stay the same. Build no longer needs the 28 px graph; keep the 24 and 32 px warm-up and capture, and keep error_on_recompile on after build.
Expected (from the queue): Time falls about 8% (2.7 epochs move from about 0.71 to 0.51 s/epoch, saving about 0.5 s), to about 6.0-6.15 s. Accuracy changes by -0.1 to -0.5 pp, to about 0.753-0.756. A loss of 0.3 pp or less beats the epoch dial (about 1.9 s/pp) and supports the tail-sets-accuracy reading of E011. A loss above 0.5 pp means the middle phase matters too, so resizing should stop here.

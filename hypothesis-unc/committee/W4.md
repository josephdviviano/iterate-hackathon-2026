W4 after E005. Results: E001 (9 epochs, eager): 0.7430 at 9.43 s. E002 (10 epochs): 0.7495 at 10.55 s. E003 (compile, first try): 0.7486 at 12.07 s. E004 (compile with channels_last warmup fix, 9 epochs): 0.7486 at 9.04 s. E005 (E004 plus Lookahead/EMA): 0.7583 at 9.10 s. E005 is the only feasible run.

Beliefs:
1. Epochs dominate time (H1). About 1.0 s per epoch with small fixed cost. Evidence: E001 and E002. Compile saved only about 4% once the stride-guard recompile was fixed (E003 vs E004), so the E003 slowdown was recompile leakage, which matches my earlier guess. Systems gains are now small, and the step is mostly compute-bound.
2. EMA/Lookahead helped far more than I predicted: +0.97 pp for about 0.06 s. I called it neutral and missed. Part of the gain may be seed luck, and the 40-seed mean is probably 0.1-0.3 pp below the 3-seed mean. The margin over 0.753 is about 0.5 pp, which is thin but real.
3. Noise. Per-seed accuracy std is about 0.3 pp, so the 3-seed mean std is about 0.15-0.2 pp. The identical-math pairs in E003/E004 matched exactly (0.7486), which suggests the runs are close to deterministic for a fixed seed. Time noise is about 1-3%, around 0.1-0.2 s.
4. Accuracy per epoch is about 0.65 pp near 9-10 epochs. With EMA the margin could be traded for roughly 0.5-0.7 epoch, which is worth 0.5-0.7 s, but it is risky for the 40-seed judgment.
5. Progressive resizing (I008): the conv FLOP saving at 24/28/32 px is about 24%, but the real saving is less because of memory-bound ops and eager overhead. Low-res early training at a 9-epoch budget probably costs 0.3-1 pp, and the margin is thin, so I expect it to fall below 0.753.
6. Muon and proxy selection are untuned and likely to lose accuracy or time.

Unsure: how much of the EMA gain is real, the true fixed-cost share, whether resizing recompiles leak into timed code, and how the accuracy-vs-epoch slope behaves with EMA.

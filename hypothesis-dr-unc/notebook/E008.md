# E008 · I011 · H3.2.1

H3.2.1 (open, c=0.30): Training the first ~50% of epochs at 24 px and the rest at 32 px cuts time by >= 10% compared with a 32 px run of equal epoch count, with accuracy loss <= 0.3 pp. Compiled graphs for both resolutions are built in build.
H3.2 (open, c=0.35): Progressive resizing: training the early epochs at reduced resolution (e.g. 24 px) costs little final accuracy, because early epochs learn coarse features, and it cuts their FLOPs by about 44%.
H3 (open, c=0.35): Doing less compute per epoch beats running fewer epochs: hard-example filtering and progressive resizing cut FLOPs per useful gradient enough to lower time-to-75% on CIFAR-100, even in a short, under-fitting schedule.
Idea: Explore. Rewrite of I009 onto the current best recipe. Start from E004 (10 epochs, no cutout). Change only the resolution schedule: for the first 50% of steps, downsample each augmented batch to 24x24 (F.interpolate, bilinear, antialias=False) after flip and translate. The remaining steps run at 32 px. Keep the LR schedule tied to step count. Compile and capture CUDA graphs for both input shapes on synthetic data in build so no recompile lands in timed train. Eval stays at 32 px.
Expected (from the queue): Time falls 10-18% (~0.9-1.5 s, to ~7.5-8.0 s). Accuracy drops 0.2-0.8 pp. A drop of <= 0.3 pp supports H3.2.1 and gives a recipe that can afford +1 epoch while still being faster. A drop of > 0.6 pp, or recompile stalls inside the timed train, refutes it.

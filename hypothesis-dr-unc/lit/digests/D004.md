# Digest: why E009 (narrow net, 12 epochs) fell short

**Result.** E009 used a 128/256/384 net for 12 epochs on the E004 recipe. It reached 0.7456, below the predicted range of [0.749, 0.761], in 7.44 s. E007 used the same widths for 10 epochs and reached 0.7402. The two extra epochs therefore added only about 0.27 pp per epoch. H1.1.2 assumed 0.4–1.0 pp per epoch. W7, W8 and W9 all treat accuracy as log(steps) plus a separate width term, so each predicted that extra epochs would buy back the narrow net's lost accuracy. They did not.

**What the cards say.**

- **C0037 (contradicts W7/W8/W9).** Accuracy scales with training time and with model size by *different* power laws. The two axes do not add independently, so the models' form, log(steps) + width, is too simple. In the slow-returns regime, the width penalty can't be traded for steps at a fixed rate. *Action:* add a steps × width interaction term to the world models before predicting more narrow-net runs.
- **C0039 (explains E007/E009).** Early in training, the cost of being narrow is about 1/width. Late in training it becomes width^-c, a different form. If our 10–12 epoch runs are in that late phase, the narrow net's deficit *grows* with more epochs instead of shrinking. That matches the flat return from 10 to 12 epochs. *Action:* treat as the working explanation. The test is to compare narrow and wide at matched epochs, e.g. both at 10, and check whether the gap widens with epochs.
- **C0038 (conditional).** At a fixed compute budget, the theory says to grow steps faster than parameters. That is H1.2's premise. But it assumes the compute-optimal regime with fresh data. Here we reuse 50k images for 12+ epochs, and E009 says the premise does not carry over at this width cut. It does not refute H1.2 as a whole. A milder cut might still sit in the regime where trading width for steps pays, e.g. 128/320/480 or 128/384/448, keeping the last block wider.
- **C0040 (untested mechanism).** The train-test gap grows with each reuse of the data, whatever the model's capacity. E004 removed cutout and lowered label smoothing to 0.1 because the wide net was under-fitting at 8.5 epochs. At 12 epochs, that light regularization may no longer be optimal: some of the extra epochs could be widening the gap instead of improving test accuracy. The tree has no hypothesis linking the best regularization strength to epoch count. *Action:* add one under H4.1, e.g. that at 12 or more epochs, cutout of about 8 px recovers at least 0.3 pp. Test it on the narrow net.
- **C0036, C0041–C0043.** These are too general or about generative pretraining. Not relevant here.

**Implications.** H1.2.1 is still open, but leaning negative. Even if the narrow net's slope held at about 0.27 pp per epoch, it would need roughly 15 epochs to reach 0.753. That costs about 9.3 s, slower than E004's 8.94 s. Downgrade the confidence of H1.2.1. The next width test should be the milder cut described under C0038, or the narrow net with mild cutout added back (C0040). Running more epochs on 128/256/384 is not worth it.

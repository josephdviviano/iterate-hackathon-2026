## Muon-family levers for very short convnet training: what the cards say

**Coverage gap first.** All 21 cards are about the Newton–Schulz (NS) orthogonalization step: its coefficients, how many iterations it runs, and cheaper replacements. None of them covers momentum or lr scaling with batch size, weight decay under Muon, schedule or warmup shape, which parameters Muon should cover, NorMuon/AdaMuon/Dion, or how Muon interacts with Lookahead, EMA or label smoothing. Nothing here explains E033 (lr 0.32 was worse) or E035 (removing Lookahead hurt). Those questions are still open in the literature we have.

**Better NS coefficients at the same cost (untested here).** On NanoGPT, polynomials chosen to maximize the derivative at zero speed up the convergence of small singular values. At an equal budget of 12 matmuls they beat Muon's default quintic on test loss (C0094, C0095, C0099). Tolerating some orthogonalization error is part of how they get that speed (C0096). The tree has no node for this. It is the only lever on the list that is zero-cost per step and aimed at accuracy per step. **Action:** add a Lvl3 under H4.2. On E031, swap the NS coefficients for CANS/Polar-Express-style per-iteration coefficients at the same iteration count, and change nothing else. All the evidence comes from transformers, so expect a small effect. Any gain under ~0.3 pp needs the H1.1.3 rerun-and-pool rule.

**Fewer NS iterations as a time lever (untested here).** One iteration, or a few, may keep most of the update's quality (C0104, C0105). On LM tasks, Hybrid-AuON beat Muon (C0108). Here, Muon's per-step cost is visible: the fused version took 6.97 s versus 6.45 s for the SGD tip E024 (E026 vs E024), about 0.5 s on a ~6 s run. This is resolution-independent per-step work, so it fits H2. **Action:** after the coefficient test, try 3 iterations with aggressive coefficients and expect about 0.1–0.25 s saved at flat accuracy. Profile the NS share first (H2.4.3).

**Conditional only:**
- C0097: tighter orthogonalization matters more for larger models. That suggests our small net tolerates looser NS, which supports the iteration cut.
- C0100, C0101: adaptive NS depth helps 160M–1B GPTs. That is far from this regime, so treat it as low priority.
- C0092: IFNSO "wins" in ≤10 epochs, but on MNIST at batch 32 with a +0.04 pp gap. That is not evidence for this loop.
- C0090, C0093, C0102, C0103: savings that only matter for large matrices or tiling. Our block-3 filters are small, so they are irrelevant at this size.

**Not relevant:** C0106, C0107, C0109 and C0110 (AuON's cosh damping) target exploding attention logits. This net has no attention and has shown no instability.

**No card contradicts a current belief.** The digest does not change the H4.2 confidences. It only adds one zero-cost accuracy test and one time test.

## Claims
- C0095 (untested; H4.2): New Lvl3: swap the NS coefficients for CANS/Polar-Express ones at equal iteration count on E031. This is a zero-cost attempt to gain accuracy per step. Evidence is from NanoGPT only.
- C0094 (untested; H4.2): Mechanism: a higher derivative at zero speeds up small-singular-value directions. This may be the route to more accuracy per epoch at no extra time.
- C0099 (untested; H4.2): Composing polynomials of different degrees gives a monotone, faster-converging map. Use it as the coefficient source for the H4.2 swap test.
- C0096 (conditional; H4.2): Accepting some orthogonalization error is part of how fast coefficients work. Allow it in the swap test and do not insist on exact orthogonality.
- C0104 (untested; H2, H2.4.3): NS iterations are resolution-independent per-step work (~0.5 s, E026 vs E024). Test 3 iterations instead of 5 for a 0.1-0.25 s time cut. Profile the NS share first.
- C0105 (untested; H2, H4.2): Few-iteration hybrids keep stability. This supports cutting NS iterations without losing accuracy.
- C0108 (conditional; H4.2): Hybrid-AuON beat Muon only on LM tasks, so it is weak support for cheaper orthogonalization in a convnet.
- C0097 (conditional; H4.2, H2): Tight orthogonalization matters more for large models. A small convnet should tolerate looser or fewer NS steps.
- C0101 (conditional; H4.2): Adaptive NS depth helps 160M-1B GPTs. Low priority here.
- C0092 (conditional; H4.2): The IFNSO short-training gain is +0.04 pp on MNIST at batch 32. Do not treat it as evidence for this regime.

## Progressive resizing (24→32 px): literature vs E008

**Premise correction:** E008 (I011) already ran this exact change; its verdict on H3.2.1 was inconclusive. It took **7.52 s / 0.7495**, vs E004 at **8.94 s / 0.7550**: about 16% faster for about −0.55 pp. The time part of H3.2.1 was met; the accuracy part (≤0.3 pp loss) was not. E008 says it starts from "E004 (10 epochs)" but E004's own description says 8.5 epochs. If E008 really ran 10 epochs, the speed gain is larger and the accuracy loss worse than it looks. Check the commit first.

**C0024 (EfficientNetV2), explains E008:** training at a growing image size speeds training up but costs accuracy, which matches E008. It was shown with full-length schedules, not ~10-epoch runs.

**C0025 (EfficientNetV2), untested here:** the accuracy loss is recovered by tying regularization to resolution (weaker augmentation at small sizes). Our loop never coupled augmentation to the resolution phase; translate 4 at 32 px became a relatively larger shift at 24 px. Next step: one run with translate at about 3 px or off during the 24 px phase.

**C0029 / C0031 / C0033 (resizing filters), untested mechanism:** bilinear downsampling without anti-aliasing aliases and gives roughly ±10% brightness error that depends on pixel offset. These papers measured image quality, not classifier accuracy. My inference (not from the papers): the frozen whitening filters are fit on 32 px statistics, so aliased 24 px inputs may suffer a mismatch on top of the lost resolution. The tree has no hypothesis for this. Cheap check: `antialias=True` or area resampling.

**C0034 / C0035 (UltraLight+), weak conditional support:** faster convergence from growing the resolution on CIFAR-10, but unquantified and mixed in with architecture changes.

C0026–C0028 (histopathology), C0030 and C0032 are not relevant.

**Bearing:** H3.2 is not refuted. W2 estimates about 0.9 pp per epoch, so buying back 0.55 pp takes about 0.6 extra epochs (+0.4–0.5 s). That predicts a feasible run near 8.0 s, about 10% under E004. Suggested order: (1) E008 with about 0.6–1 more epochs; (2) anti-aliased resize; (3) translate weakened only in the low-res phase; (4) a 30% low-res fraction if still short. Treat accuracy differences below about 0.3 pp as noise; E001's repeat-run noise check was never reported.

## Claims
- C0024 (explains; H3.2, H3.2.1): Explains E008: progressive resizing gave about 16% less time and about -0.55 pp accuracy. Treat the accuracy loss as an expected cost, not a refutation of H3.2; compensate with about 0.6-1 extra epochs.
- C0025 (untested; H3.2, H4.1): Mechanism missing from the tree: tie augmentation strength to resolution. Test translate at about 3 px or off during the 24 px phase, everything else as in E008.
- C0029 (untested; H3.2.1): Downsampling with antialias=False aliases; this may combine with the frozen 32 px whitening filters to cost accuracy (inference, not from the paper). Test antialias=True or area resampling, a change that should cost almost nothing per step.
- C0031 (untested; H3.2.1): Linear-kernel downsampling gives roughly ±10% brightness error that depends on pixel offset, which may shift low-res input statistics away from the whitening fit (inference). Supports trying a different resize mode.
- C0034 (conditional; H3.2): Weak CIFAR-10 support for faster convergence from growing the resolution; unquantified and mixed in with architecture changes. Give it little weight.

## E019 (96/256/576): why the block-1 cut lost 0.52 pp

**Result.** Compared with E012 (128/256/576, 11 ep), E019 lost 0.52 pp (0.7567 → 0.7515) and saved 0.74 s (−9.6%). That is ~0.70 pp per second saved. The resize lever cost 0.41 pp/s (E013), and dropping one epoch from E012 cost 0.74 pp/s (E020: −0.51 pp for −0.69 s). So the block-1 cut costs accuracy at about the same rate as dropping epochs. It is not a "free" FLOP cut. W21 actually predicted the direction ("esp. stage 1/3"). W19 and W20 treated stage-1 width mainly as a time cost.

**What the cards say.**

- **C0046** (ImageNet ResNet18/MobileNetV2, width search at full training budgets): the best width allocation puts disproportionate channels in the final layers. This is the prior behind "early width is cheap", and E019 contradicts it here. The card's regime is converged training. Our loop under-fits at ~11 epochs, and there block 1 is the first *learned* layer after the frozen whitening, at full 32×32 resolution. Its width can limit how fast useful features form, not just the final ceiling. Treat C0046 as **conditional** on long budgets. Don't extend it to sub-converged runs.
- **C0048** (ImageNet, transferring widths across resolution): narrowing blocks unevenly, without keeping the cross-stage width ratios coordinated, costs more accuracy than per-block estimates predict. E019 stacks a second uneven cut (block 1) on top of E012's block-2 cut, giving a 96→256→576 profile. The card's mechanism, block-structure coherence, is **untested** in our tree. The evidence is indirect: it comes from a different task and from transfer experiments, not from ablations.
- **C0047** (relative widths within a stage transfer across depth): weakly relevant. It does say within-stage ratios can be kept while depth changes, which bears on H1.2.3 (2-conv blocks). **Conditional**.
- **C0049–C0052** (GoogLeNet design principles): too generic. Nothing usable.

**Caveats before attributing a mechanism.**

1. The E019 description says "translate 4", but E020 describes the E012 commit as translate 2. Check the diff. A translate confound would be about the size of E011's effect.
2. The noise floor (H1.1.3) is still unmeasured. A 0.52 pp gap on 3 seeds may be only ~2σ.

**Actions.**

- Fold the observation into H1.2 as a mechanism: in the under-fitting regime, block-1 width trades accuracy at about the epoch rate, so cutting it gains nothing.
- Add a Lvl3 test of C0048's coherence idea: proportional 112/320/640-style scaling vs uneven cuts at matched FLOPs.
- Deprioritize further block-1 cuts. Spend width where E021 showed it pays (block 3).

## Claims
- C0046 (conditional; H1.2, H5.1): Late layers needing more width holds for converged ImageNet training. It does not license cheap early-width cuts in the ~11-epoch under-fitting regime, where E019 lost 0.52 pp. Don't use it to justify block-1 narrowing.
- C0048 (untested; H1.2, H1.2.2): Possible mechanism: uneven cuts across stages cost more than per-block estimates. Test proportional vs uneven width profiles at matched FLOPs.
- C0047 (conditional; H1.2.3): Within-stage width ratios transfer across depth. Weak support for trying 2-conv blocks while keeping widths fixed.

## Digest: fast CIFAR-100 recipes, short-regime trade-offs

**Short answer: the verified cards don't answer the question.** None of them report CIFAR-100 results, epochs-to-75%, width sweeps, or label-smoothing/cutout/translate strengths below about 15 epochs. Nothing here compares whitened CNNs with ResNet-18-class nets on CIFAR-100. So the starting recipe still rests on the unverified sources already in the tree (futurebiohackers PR #3: airbench96 reached 75.6% at 9 epochs), not on this batch.

**What the cards do support:**

- **C0001** (airbench: 94% on CIFAR-10 in 3.29 s on an A100). *Conditional.* It supports H1's choice of architecture family, but only for CIFAR-10. It says nothing about the CIFAR-100 epoch count in H1.1/H1.1.2. Use it as the reason to start from airbench96, not as evidence for the 8.5-epoch edge.
- **C0002** (derandomized flip beats random flip whenever flipping helps; CIFAR-10). *Conditional.* The card is mapped to H1.2, but it really bears on **H4.3/H4.3.1**. Make alternating flip the default in the first recipe, since it costs nothing. Test H4.3.1 later as an ablation (switch back to random flip) rather than as an add-on.
- **C0006** (how low the LR ends matters only over short horizons). *Conditional* on our ~8-epoch regime, which is exactly where it applies. For the **H1.1.2** sweep, scale the schedule so it anneals to its endpoint at every epoch count. Otherwise the accuracy-vs-epochs curve mixes epoch effects with schedule effects, and interpolating along it is invalid.
- **C0005/C0004** (schedule shape matters more than its parameters; smooth schedules are statistically tied; MNIST/CIFAR-10). *Conditional.* Use one smooth schedule (airbench's linear warmup and decay) and don't spend early runs tuning it.
- **C0008** (which LR scheduler is best depends strongly on architecture; CIFAR-10). *Untested here.* It suggests that ResNet schedule lore won't carry over to the whitened net. This mildly supports H4's premise that CIFAR-10 settings need re-tuning. It doesn't say which direction to tune.

C0003, C0007, C0009 and C0010 are about schedule benchmarks at much lower accuracy (CIFAR-10 at 80–86%). They don't bear on this loop.

**For the loop:**
1. E000 shows the template is a stub. **H2.1.1's 50–70 s baseline premise is false**, so retire or rewrite it. Measure the GPU-pipeline gain against the first real recipe instead.
2. Build the first recipe as airbench96 128/384/576 with alternating flip and a smooth schedule that anneals fully. Run the H1.1.1 reproduction and the H1.1.2 sweep at 7, 8.5 and 10 epochs.
3. H1.2 (width) and H4.1 (weaker regularization) have **no literature support either way**. Settle them empirically once the feasible edge is known. Consider a targeted search for CIFAR-100 airbench and ResNet-18 short-schedule ablations.

## Claims
- C0001 (conditional; H1, H1.1): Supports the airbench family only on CIFAR-10. Start from airbench96, but get the CIFAR-100 epoch edge from the H1.1.1/H1.1.2 runs.
- C0002 (conditional; H4.3, H4.3.1): Card is mapped to H1.2 but bears on H4.3. Default to alternating flip in the first recipe and test H4.3.1 as an ablation back to random flip.
- C0006 (conditional; H1.1.2): The LR endpoint matters in short (~8 epoch) runs. Scale the schedule to anneal fully at each swept epoch count so the accuracy-vs-epochs curve can be interpolated.
- C0005 (conditional; H1.1.2, H4): Schedule shape matters more than its parameters. Keep one smooth schedule and don't spend early budget tuning it.
- C0004 (conditional; H4): Smooth schedules are statistically tied on CIFAR-10. Switching between them is unlikely to beat the 3-seed noise floor.
- C0008 (untested; H4): The best scheduler depends on architecture. Mild support for H4's premise that ResNet/CIFAR-10 schedule settings won't carry over; doesn't say which direction to tune.

## Muon at 9.5 epochs: what the literature says

**Bottom line.** The cards back the idea behind the proposal: Muon's gain is better accuracy per sample, not per second, so the natural way to cash it in is to cut epochs. None of the cards tests a small CNN at about 10 epochs, though, and none compares Muon with Nesterov SGD on a convnet. The size of the trade has to come from the run itself.

**Mechanism.** Orthogonalizing the update flattens its spectrum. Muon's stable step size then scales with the *average* singular value of the gradient, not the largest one (C0062, C0063). As a result it reaches accuracy milestones "several epochs earlier" even at the same step size (C0064). That is a horizontal shift of the accuracy-vs-epochs curve, and it is exactly what the proposal spends. C0068 says the advantage is in samples (tokens), not wall-clock. That **explains E025**: +0.74 pp over E023 but +0.61 s (≈9% overhead from Newton–Schulz). At equal epochs Muon loses on time, so it can only win through fewer epochs.

**Rough arithmetic.** E025 clears the 0.753 line by 0.92 pp. Scaling 7.26 s by 9.5/11 gives about 6.3 s, under the E024 tip (6.45 s). The SGD slope (0.1–0.5 pp/epoch near 11 epochs, from E017/E020) is the wrong slope to use: under the shift Muon's own curve sits lower and may be steeper. Run the 9.5-epoch point as planned and report per-seed accuracy. If the margin is under ~0.3 pp, rerun and pool (H1.1.3).

**Conditional.** C0058 says Muon's efficiency gain is roughly constant across training horizon. That would mean the +0.74 pp survives at 9.5 epochs. But it was measured against AdamW on LLMs (51M–253M parameters), not against SGD on a 10-epoch CNN. C0053 and C0056 say the best hyperparameters and momentum memory change with horizon, and shorter runs favour shorter memory. So lr 0.24 and momentum 0.6, carried over from airbench94_muon's CIFAR-10 setting, may be off at 9.5 epochs. After the epoch cut, a small sweep over Muon lr (0.24 vs ~0.35, which Muon's stability allows per C0065) and momentum (0.6 vs 0.5) is the obvious next step.

**Untested (missing from the tree):**
- **Augmentation interaction.** C0074, C0075 and C0077 report that Muon benefits much *more* than the baseline optimizer from heavy augmentation, and that removing it causes late-training spectral collapse. H4.1's "weaker regularization is better at about 10 epochs" was learned under SGD (E004, E011). Add a child under H4.2: on the Muon base, putting cutout 8 or translate 4 back gains ≥0.3 pp.
- **Layer-localized benefit.** C0067 finds Muon's benefit concentrated in particular layers (in Mamba, the output projection). Here that means testing Muon on block 3 only, or blocks 2–3 only, which could also recover part of the 0.6 s Newton–Schulz overhead.

**H4.2 needs updating.** E025 refuted the "Muon loses to SGD" prior that H4.2 still carries at confidence 0.25. Raise its confidence and rewrite it as an epochs-for-time lever.

The momentum-ordering theory cards (C0059–C0061) say nothing about the current recipe and are not used.

## Claims
- C0064 (explains; H4.2, H4.2.1, H1.1): Muon reaches milestones several epochs earlier: E025's +0.74 pp is a horizontal shift of the epoch curve, which justifies cutting 11 to 9.5 epochs; measure Muon's own slope, not the SGD slope.
- C0068 (explains; H4.2, H2): Muon's advantage is in samples, not wall-clock; matches E025's +0.61 s overhead at equal epochs, so Muon only pays through fewer epochs.
- C0062 (explains; H4.2): Spectral flattening is the mechanism behind Muon's faster per-sample convergence.
- C0063 (conditional; H4.2, H4.5): Stable step size scales with the mean singular value, so a higher Muon lr than 0.24 may be usable; sweep it after the epoch cut.
- C0058 (conditional; H4.2, H1.1): Constant Muon gain across horizon (LLMs, vs AdamW) suggests the +0.74 pp survives at 9.5 epochs; unverified for SGD on a 10-epoch CNN.
- C0053 (conditional; H4.5, H4.2): Best hyperparameters shift with horizon; retune Muon lr and momentum at 9.5 epochs rather than inheriting CIFAR-10 airbench94_muon values.
- C0056 (conditional; H4.2, H4.5): Shorter horizons favour shorter optimizer memory; try Muon momentum 0.5 vs 0.6 at 9.5 epochs.
- C0065 (conditional; H4.2): Muon stays stable at LRs where SGD diverges; supports trying lr around 0.35 at the shorter budget.
- C0074 (untested; H4.1, H4.2): Muon gains more from heavy augmentation; H4.1's light-regularization finding was learned under SGD. Add a test that restores cutout 8 or translate 4 on the Muon base.
- C0075 (untested; H4.1, H4.2): Without augmentation Muon shows late-training spectral collapse; possible reason the Muon recipe wants regularization back.
- C0077 (untested; H4.1, H4.2): Muon is an optimizer-recipe interaction; the regularization optimum should be re-measured under Muon.
- C0067 (untested; H4.2, H1.2): Muon's benefit is layer-localized; test Muon on block 3 only (or blocks 2-3) to keep the gain while cutting Newton-Schulz overhead.

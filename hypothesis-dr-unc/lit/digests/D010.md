## Polar Express Newton-Schulz coefficients on the Muon tip (E031)

**Bottom line:** the literature suggests this is more likely a small time lever than an accuracy lever. On accuracy, expect |Δ| under the noise floor (~0.3 pp, H1.1.3).

**What the papers say**
- **C0111, C0112 (conditional):** Per-iteration (Polar Express) coefficients get closer to the polar factor than the fixed quintic. On synthetic matrices with singular values spanning 1e-3 to 1e3, they need about half as many iterations. The reason is that the fixed polynomial starts slowly: small singular values are under-amplified in the first iterations. If our count really is 3 (confirm in code), the approximation gap is at its largest, so a measurable effect is most plausible here. But these are approximation-error results, not training results.
- **C0113, C0114 (contradicts the "accuracy lever" premise in the proposal):** In GPT-2 Small and in 1–4B MoE/Mamba models, training quality is not monotonic in how accurately the polar factor is computed. Truncated Polar Express, the Jordan quintic, cubic NS and FP32 SVD all reach "nearly indistinguishable" loss (~1e-3). Nobody has tested small conv filters at ~170 steps. Still, the prior for a ≥0.3 pp gain is low. Preregister it as a likely null.
- **C0118 (untested mechanism):** Under one theoretical reading, the NS coefficients act as right-preconditioning plus a dynamic L2 regularizer. In practice, changing the coefficients changes the singular-value profile of the update. The Jordan quintic leaves singular values roughly in 0.7–1.1, while Polar Express pushes them toward 1. That shifts the update RMS, which works like a hidden change to the Muon LR. E033 showed this loop is LR-sensitive: lr 0.24→0.32 cost 0.33 pp. Without matching the update RMS, any result is confounded with an LR change. Normalize the update to E031's RMS, or log the RMS for both variants.
- **C0115, C0116 (conditional, time):** Cubic NS (10 vs 15 matmuls) matched quintic quality. Combined with Polar Express's faster convergence, this suggests a variant that **saves time**: fewer iterations or a cheaper polynomial at equal quality. This is the more promising direction, because W31–W33 all put a fixed Muon overhead into per-step cost. The saving only matters if NS is a measurable share of a step after the E026 fusion. Profile it first (ties to H2.4.3).
- **C0120, C0121 (conditional):** On simple stochastic problems, Muon's convergence depends on batch size in theory. This loosely fits E032 (batch 3072 lost ~1.3 pp), but those are theory results on toy problems, so don't use them as an explanation.

**Suggested action**
1. Add an H4.2 Lvl3: "Polar Express at the same iteration count, with update RMS matched, changes 3-seed accuracy by <0.3 pp."
2. Add a sibling about time: "Polar Express with one fewer NS iteration (or cubic) keeps accuracy within noise and cuts time by ≥ the NS share." Run it only if profiling shows NS takes ≥2% of the step.

## Claims
- C0111 (conditional; H4.2, H4.2.1): Polar Express converges about 2x faster to the polar factor, but only shown on synthetic matrices. At our ~3 NS iterations the approximation gap is largest, so any effect is most likely here. It is not evidence of a training gain.
- C0112 (untested; H4.2): Mechanism: fixed NS under-amplifies small singular values in early iterations (slow start). Whether that matters is decided by how much the low-singular-value directions in conv-filter updates contribute at ~170 steps.
- C0114 (contradicts; H4.2): Training quality is not monotonic in polar accuracy, which undercuts the 'zero-cost accuracy lever' premise. Preregister |dAcc| < 0.3 pp as the expected outcome.
- C0113 (contradicts; H4.2): Cubic and quintic NS reach nearly identical loss (LLM scale), so a coefficient swap should not move accuracy much.
- C0118 (untested; H4.2, H4.1): Coefficients act as preconditioning plus implicit regularization. A swap changes update RMS, i.e. it is a hidden Muon-LR change (E033 shows LR sensitivity). Match update RMS to E031, or log it for both variants.
- C0115 (conditional; H4.2, H2.4): Cubic NS cuts matmuls by 33% at parity. Combined with Polar Express, fewer or cheaper iterations is a time lever, worth testing only if a profile shows NS is a measurable share of the fused step.
- C0116 (conditional; H4.2): Supports a sibling Lvl3: a cheaper orthogonalization at equal accuracy, aimed at time rather than accuracy.
- C0120 (conditional; H4.2, H4.4): Theory says Muon's convergence depends on batch size. Only a loose fit to E032 (batch 3072 failed), toy-problem theory, so do not use it as an explanation.
